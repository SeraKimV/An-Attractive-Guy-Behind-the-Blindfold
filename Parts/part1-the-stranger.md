# Part 1 — The Stranger (COMPLETE)

**Recap:** Gojo and Sara meet by chance on a Tokyo street — his cursed energy reacts to her before he understands why, and she just thinks he's an attractive stranger. He gives his ridiculous self-introduction, she turns him down for the library, then surprises them both by asking for his number and telling him he feels "warm" and "reliable." Sara turns out to already know Yuji online (from Korea); Yuji recognizes Gojo from a distance and is stunned by the coincidence. Their first group outing (ramen + kikufuku, with Yuji as an unplanned chaperone) establishes the food-banter dynamic and Sara's clumsiness (losing her keys at the guesthouse gate). Their first just-the-two-of-them outing follows: enrollment day, jiggly pancakes, a hometown conversation where Sara describes her temple-filled town south of Seoul (setup for later), and a near-fall at the Mitaka observatory where Gojo catches her — ending on a Mars/Milky Way viewing and a curfew sprint.

**Open threads going into Part 2:** the "warmth" comment still lingers unexplained for both of them; Gojo hasn't started investigating Sara yet; no dreams have started yet; the blindfold has only been noticed as a visual curiosity, not yet as a symbol Sara consciously wants to look behind.

# Chapter 1 \- The Girl Who Made Him Look Twice

(Satoru’s POV)

Tokyo was loud tonight.

Yuji was talking. Megumi was complaining. Nobara looked like she was already regretting agreeing to dinner.

Satoru Gojo was only half listening.

A bag of mochi sat beside him as he leaned back in his chair, cheek resting lazily against one hand.

"So, Gojo-sensei," Yuji said, turning toward him with the kind of enthusiasm that should have been illegal after a mission, "any pointers? For real ones. Not the 'believe in yourself' kind."

Megumi scoffed. "First you bench me the entire mission, and now you want to give advice like you actually watched."

Satoru grinned. "You'll need to learn to share, Megumi. You're not an only \-"

He stopped.

Something had changed.

It wasn't loud. No sudden disturbance. No curse he could immediately identify.

Just \-

A scent. Vanilla. Something floral beneath it. Mimosa, maybe. Cherry blossoms, softened by the evening air.

And underneath the scent, something else. A faint pull.

His cursed energy stirred before his mind caught up, humming against something he couldn't see.

Satoru's hand moved to his blindfold. He lifted it.

*Rare. Very rare.*

His uncovered eye scanned the street, then found her.

A girl walking through the crowd, a bag hanging from one shoulder, her attention fixed somewhere ahead. Dark hair. Tired posture. The unmistakable look of someone trying to get somewhere while thinking about ten other things at the same time.

Nothing about her looked unusual.

And yet \- his cursed energy reacted again.

He stared.

"Gojo-sensei?" Nobara's voice barely registered. "You good?"

Satoru didn't answer. For four seconds, maybe five, the world had narrowed to one person he'd never seen before.

Then he lowered the blindfold. He smiled.

"Yeah. I'm fine."

It was the easiest lie in the world. He wasn't.

Nobody caught his attention like this. Not like that.

People usually saw the blindfold first. Then the reputation. The strongest. The Six Eyes. The sorcerer they either feared, admired, or wanted something from.

But this girl—he didn't know what she'd seen. He didn't even know what he'd sensed. His cursed energy had recognized something before he had. Something about her. Something it shouldn't have recognized at all.

His smile faded beneath the blindfold.

*Who are you?*

He stood.

"Where are you going?" Yuji asked.

"Nowhere."

That was clearly a lie. Satoru was already walking.

He crossed the distance without really deciding to. She noticed him before he reached her \- hard not to. He was tall enough to stand out in any crowd, white hair catching the city lights, black blindfold making him even more impossible to ignore.

She slowed. Looked up.

For just a second, surprise crossed her face. Then curiosity. No fear.

*Interesting.*

Satoru stopped a few feet away. "Hi."

She blinked. "Hi."

He smiled. "New around here?"

She hesitated, then nodded. "I'm Sara Kang. I came here to study from South Korea."

There was something almost endearingly direct about the way she said it. No dramatic reaction. No recognition. No nervous rambling. Just an introduction.

"And you are?"

Satoru tilted his head. There it was. She genuinely didn't know. He couldn't decide whether that amused him or intrigued him more.

"Sara Kang, huh?" he said. "All the way from South Korea just to study? Bold choice."

A small smile appeared on her face.

He continued, because obviously he couldn't help himself. "I hope your textbooks are ready for a lot of distractions." He folded his arms. "As for me? I'm the best thing you'll see in this city. Satoru Gojo. Teacher, genius, and officially the most handsome guy at Jujutsu High."

He paused. "Since you're new, I could give you a tour. I'm much more interesting than a library."

She stared at him for a second, then glanced at the time. "Thanks for the offer, but I actually need to get to the library."

"Oh."

"Now."

"Wow."

She started walking. "Bye, Gojo."

Satoru watched her go. Most people would have stopped. Most people would have laughed at his joke. Most people would have looked back.

She did none of those things. Almost none.

Just before disappearing into the crowd, she turned her head. "Actually \- "

He raised an eyebrow.

"Can I get your number?"

Satoru froze. For perhaps the second time that evening, he genuinely didn't have a response ready.

Then his grin returned. "Oh, you're bold."

He pulled out his phone and held it toward her. "Most people are too intimidated to even make eye contact with me, and here you are asking the strongest sorcerer alive for his number like it's nothing."

She entered her number. He watched her fingers move across the screen \- too carefully, too interested. He didn't comment on it. Yet.

She handed the phone back. Their fingers brushed. A brief touch.

He could have let Infinity handle it. Instead, he didn't.

For half a second, he let himself feel the warmth of another person's hand. Ordinary. Simple. Strangely grounding.

Sara withdrew first. "I don't know why," she said softly.

Satoru looked at her. She seemed almost confused by her own words.

"I find you reliable, somehow."

He blinked. "Reliable?"

She gave a tiny, embarrassed laugh. "Yeah. You give off this... warmth, I guess." She looked down for a moment. "And I've never really trusted that from a stranger before."

The teasing reply he'd been preparing disappeared.

*Warmth.*

Nobody called him that. People called him terrifying. Powerful. Untouchable. The strongest. Never warm.

For one brief moment, he didn't know what to say. Then he smiled again.

"You really are interesting, Sara Kang."

Her smile widened. "Goodbye, Gojo."

"See you soon."

She paused. "I didn't say I'll see you again."

Satoru's grin sharpened. "You gave me your number."

She laughed, then disappeared into the crowd.

He remained where he was for another few seconds. Behind him, Nobara finally caught up.

"She energetic?"

Satoru slipped his phone into his pocket. "Yeah." His smile lingered. "She is."

# Chapter 2 \- The Big Guy Who Had Warmth

(Sara's POV)

Sara had exactly one goal for the evening: find the university library before it closed.

Apparently, Tokyo had other plans.

Starting with a six-foot-something stranger in a black blindfold who had stopped her in the middle of the sidewalk like he owned the pavement underneath him.

She kept walking toward the station, but her mind was stubbornly stuck on the encounter.

*Satoru Gojo.*

He had introduced himself like she was supposed to recognize his name instantly.

She didn't.

Yet somehow, she'd still given him her number.

And then there was that word.

*Warmth.*

She hadn't meant to say it out loud. It had just slipped past her lips before her conscious mind could stop it.

Sara shook her head and pulled her coat tighter against the cool evening breeze.

*Get a grip, Sara. You came to Tokyo for your PhD, not to think about strange men on sidewalks.*

She picked up her pace.

She needed to get to the library.

That was all.

"SARA?\!"

She stopped.

A boy with spiky pink hair was jogging through the station crowd, looking far too excited to see her.

"Yuji?"

Sara stared at him in disbelief.

"It really is you\!" He skidded to a stop in front of her, grinning from ear to ear. "I knew you said your program was starting this month, but I didn't think I'd actually run into you in your first week\!"

Sara couldn't help laughing.

Talking to Yuji online over the past year had been surprisingly easy. What started as an accidental thread reply on a food blog had turned into regular messages \- late-night study complaints, restaurant recommendations, and his endless stories about his eccentric school life.

Seeing him in person was surreal.

He was exactly how she'd imagined him.

Bright.

Open.

And undeniably wholesome.

Just louder.

"Look at you, actually in Tokyo," Yuji said, falling into step beside her.

"Yeah. And currently trying to find the quietest corner of the library before my brain short-circuits."

"Perfect. I'm heading that way anyway."

They started walking toward the university gates.

For a few minutes, they talked about her flight, the terrifying price of Tokyo apartments, and the overwhelming size of the subway system.

Then Yuji suddenly glanced behind them.

His eyes widened.

"Wait."

Sara followed his gaze.

The tall man she'd just left was still visible in the distance.

White hair.

Black blindfold.

Hands in his pockets.

Gojo.

Yuji looked back at her.

"No way."

Sara raised an eyebrow.

"What?"

"That was Gojo-sensei, wasn't it?"

"Your teacher?"

"Yeah\!"

Yuji laughed.

"Man, of all the people in Tokyo for you to bump into first."

Sara smiled.

"He's certainly memorable."

"What did he say to you?"

"He called himself the most handsome guy in the city."

Yuji burst out laughing.

"Yep. That's definitely him."

"And he offered to show me around Tokyo."

"Did you say no?"

"I told him I had to go to the library."

Yuji stared at her.

"You rejected Gojo-sensei for textbooks."

"I didn't reject him\!" Sara protested. "I just had priorities."

Yuji shook his head dramatically.

"That's brutal, Sara."

She laughed.

They continued toward the university.

After a moment, Yuji glanced at her again.

"So... what did you think of him?"

"Gojo?"

"Yeah."

Sara thought about it.

"He's... hard to pin down."

"That's one way to describe him."

"Overconfident, obviously."

"Very."

"But there was something about him."

Yuji tilted his head.

"Something?"

Sara hesitated.

She remembered the strange calm she had felt when their fingers touched.

That feeling of being completely at ease around someone she had met only minutes ago.

"I don't know."

She looked ahead.

"He just felt... warm."

Yuji's smile softened.

"Yeah."

Sara looked at him.

"You agree?"

"Sensei acts like a total kid most of the time, but..." Yuji shrugged. "He takes care of people."

Sara nodded slowly.

"That makes sense."

"And he's the strongest guy I know."

"Strongest at what?"

Yuji's smile became slightly awkward.

"Uh..."

Sara waited.

"Is your school some kind of martial arts academy?"

"Not exactly."

He scratched the back of his neck.

"It's complicated."

He laughed.

"There are people who deal with curses and supernatural stuff. Sorcerers, basically."

Sara blinked.

"Curses?"

"Yeah. Bad supernatural things."

He made a vague gesture with his hands.

"It's a whole thing."

Sara studied him for a moment.

He didn't look like he was joking.

But he also clearly wasn't ready to explain everything.

"Okay," she said.

Yuji looked relieved.

"Okay?"

"I've been in Tokyo for less than a week. I'll allow one mysterious school."

Yuji laughed.

"Fair."

They reached the university library a few minutes later.

Inside, the grand high-ceilinged room was hushed and dimly lit.

She claimed a wide wooden table near the tall glass windows and pulled out her laptop and enrollment forms.

None of this felt familiar yet.

Starting a PhD in a foreign country meant enrollment forms, new books, research papers, induction sessions, and learning an entirely new campus and city.

Yuji dropped into the chair across from her, watching her shuffle through the papers.

"So this is your exciting PhD life?"

"Very confusing, as it's just about to begin."

She opened her laptop.

"I have to enroll in a few mandatory courses this week. And also fill out some forms, borrow a few books..."

Yuji winced.

"That looks like a lot."

They spent the next few minutes talking quietly.

Yuji told her about school.

Sara complained while filling out the forms.

They argued about which convenience store had the best snacks.

For a while, everything felt normal.

Then her phone vibrated on the wooden table.

Sara picked it up.

An unsaved number appeared on the screen.

**Unknown:** *hey. it's the strongest sorcerer alive. figured i'd save you the trouble of saving my contact yourself. 🍡✨✨*

Sara stared at the message.

Then smiled.

Across the table, Yuji noticed.

"Is that him?"

Sara locked her phone.

"Maybe."

Yuji grinned.

"Definitely him."

Her phone vibrated again.

**Gojo:** *still at the library? I know a dessert place nearby that stays open till 2 AM. just saying.*

Sara stared at the screen.

She had mandatory course forms to fill out.

She had promised herself she would spend tonight getting organized.

She was supposed to be focusing entirely on her PhD.

She typed anyway.

**Sara:** *Be my guide in this new city first, Gojo sir. Then we'll talk about desserts.*

She hit send.

Then she placed her phone face down on the table.

A smile tugged at the corner of her lips.

Maybe Tokyo wasn't going to be as quiet as she'd planned.

And somehow, she didn't mind.

# Chapter 3 \- Ramen, Kikufuku and a Chaperone

Sara shoved the last of her enrollment forms into her bag, not bothering to sort them properly.  
She'd deal with the paper chaos later.

"You're leaving already?" Yuji asked, peering over his open textbook from across the library table. "Gojo's taking me around tonight."

Yuji's face lit up instantly. "Wait. I'm coming."

"You don't have to..."

"I haven't seen you in person for five minutes and you're already trying to ditch me for my own teacher? Rude." He was already packing his backpack. "I'm coming."

Sara laughed. "Fine, fine."

Her phone buzzed on the way out of the building.

**Gojo:** *already outside. hope you walk faster than you text.*

She showed the screen to Yuji, who grinned like a kid on Christmas. "He's going to love this."

Outside, Gojo spotted them from halfway down the block \- Sara first, out of habit now, and then a half-step behind her, unmistakably his own student.

"...Itadori?"

"Gojo-sensei\!" Yuji waved like they hadn't seen each other three hours ago.

Gojo looked genuinely surprised under his blindfold.

"Couldn't believe my long-lost internet best friend met my sensei as soon as she landed in Japan," Yuji said, practically chirping. 

"I heard you'd be guiding Sara, so let's start with the food tour."

"Wonderful. A surprise chaperone." Gojo turned, waving a hand for them to follow. "Fine. Keep up, both of you."

The debate started two blocks later.

"Ramen," Yuji said. "There's a place near here that does the good stuff."

"We're getting dessert first," Gojo insisted.

"Dessert isn't a meal, sensei."

"Dessert is a lifestyle, Itadori."

They both turned to Sara at the same time, expecting her to break the tie.

"Sara, back me up," Yuji said.

"No, back me up," Gojo said. "I'm older. And better-looking."

Sara held up both hands. "We do both. Yuji first, because he's my actual friend. Dessert after, because apparently that's non-negotiable for someone, and it should ideally be eaten at the end."

Gojo pointed at her, delighted. "You are a diplomat, Ms Kang."

Yuji groaned. "You just wanted dessert either way\!"

"I wanted dessert either way," Gojo agreed cheerfully.

It was a buzzing Friday night in Tokyo \- the streets packed with people, neon signs gleaming against the damp pavement, and the cool evening air carrying the aroma of street food. They headed straight into an Ichiran ramen booth.

For Sara, taking that first sip of rich, steaming broth on a crisp Friday night was absolute perfection. Both Sara and Yuji, self-proclaimed foodies who had spent months texting about flavours, immediately appreciated the rich pork-bone broth. Soon, all three were slurping their noodles.

Over ramen, Yuji told the story of how he and Sara had met online over a year ago.

"She sent me a fifteen-minute voice message once," Yuji said. "Just complaining about a professor. I still have it saved."

"It was a very justified complaint," Sara said.

"I never said it wasn't\!"

Gojo listened more than he talked, watching the easy, uncomplicated rhythm between them.

"So where are you staying?" Yuji asked, finishing his bowl. "Since the dorms don't open till next week?"

"A guesthouse near the station," Sara said. "Traditional, kind of pretty, actually. Couldn't find a flat in time, but they lock the doors at 10pm."

"10PM," Gojo repeated. "Like a curfew. Tragic."

"It's fine. I like the walk back anyway."

When the bowls were cleared, Gojo set a box of kikufuku down \- faintly chilled, flour-dusted                                                                                                                                                                          .

"What even is this?" Sara asked.

"Kikufuku. Mochi, sweet bean paste, whipped cream. My favourite thing in this city, if you're taking notes." He nudged one toward her, his fingers brushing hers for a fraction of a second as she took it.

Neither of them mentioned the touch.

Sara took a bite, her eyes widening in surprise. "Oh, wow\! Why is this mochi so soft? The cream isn't overly sweet at all, and that matcha flavour hits right at the end... okay, this is amazing."

"Told you," Gojo said, entirely smug.

"Okay," Gojo continued, pushing the box toward the middle. "Emergency ruling. Sara's the judge. Best food of the night: ramen or kikufuku. Final answer."

"I'm literally sitting right here," Yuji complained.

"Which is why we need an unbiased judge."

Sara looked between the empty ramen bowls and the last mochi, enjoying the high stakes. "The Kikufuku wins," she announced with a grin. "Hands down."

"Traitor\!" Yuji muttered, stealing the last piece anyway.

Sara laughed. "It was close\! Both were amazing. Thank you guys, for a yummy Friday dinner."

Yuji peeled off first at the station, citing early morning training. "Get her home safe, sensei\! 10PM\! Don't make me hear about a locked gate from her tomorrow\!"

"I'm a responsible adult, Itadori."

"That's not a no\!" Yuji called back before vanishing into the crowd.

The walk to her guesthouse was quieter. Sara pointed out her gate as they arrived.

She reached into her bag for the keys. Nothing...

She checked the front pocket, then the side pocket, then the front pocket again.

"Come on..."

Gojo watched, amused, as she started searching through her bag more frantically.

"Found them," she finally said, pulling out the keys with a relieved smile.

He couldn't help smiling.

"Goodnight, Gojo."

"Goodnight, Sara."

She disappeared inside, and Gojo started walking back, pulling out his phone.

**Gojo:** *Progress. You survived curfew. Goodnight, warden\!*  
**Sara:** *Goodnight, Gojo.*  
**Gojo:** *So... tomorrow is Saturday. What kind of tourist doesn't let her guide take her on a proper tour?*  
**Sara:** *What kind of guide doesn't tell his tourist the plan?*  
**Gojo:** *The kind who keeps it a surprise. Wear comfortable shoes.*  
**Sara:** *Again? Fine. Trusting the guide. But I have some academic work to be done; I would be free by 4 PM.*  
**Gojo:** *Then let us meet around 4 PM. Goodnight Sara.*  
**Sara:** *See you tomorrow.*

Gojo looked at his screen a moment longer than he needed, sliding his phone back into his pocket with a quiet, lingering smile.

# Chapter 4 \- Mars, Pancakes and a Bad Sense of Balance

Sara lay in bed, staring at the ceiling of the guesthouse room, still thinking about the half-second his fingers had brushed hers over the kikufuku.

It wasn't a big deal. It absolutely wasn't a big deal. People brushed hands passing food all the time.

And yet she'd noticed it. And apparently her brain had decided it was worth replaying twice.

She groaned quietly and rolled onto her side, pulling up her mental to-do list instead. Forms. Books. Enrollment. Campus tour. All of it needed to happen before four, because apparently she'd agreed to "tomorrow, just the two of us" without fully registering what that meant.

"*Focus, Sara.*"

She was asleep before she found an answer.

Saturday moved fast.

By 10AM, she'd filled out three separate enrollment forms and stood in two different lines she wasn't sure she needed.

By 12PM, she'd bought half her required textbooks and borrowed the rest from the library, arms aching under the weight of it.

By 2PM, her enrollment was officially submitted, stamped, done.

Lunch was a hasty canteen sandwich, eaten standing up in the shadow of two concrete structures she still couldn't tell apart. Then she allowed herself the smallest victory lap, wandering the campus grounds with an iced coffee, finally just looking at the place she'd be spending the next several years.

Her phone buzzed at 4 PM exactly.

**Gojo:** *Ready, Ms Tourist?*  
**Sara:** *Already touring, actually. I'm ahead of schedule for once.*  
**Gojo:** *Unacceptable. Send me the location. I'm coming to fix that.*

She sent the pin. 10 minutes later, he found her near the main gate, hands in his coat pockets, scanning the ground before he even said hello.

"You wore the shoes," he said, sounding entirely too pleased about it.

"You said comfortable. I have self-preservation instincts."

"Good instincts. You'll need them tonight." He didn't elaborate. "Come on. Sweets first."

The line for the pancake shop wrapped halfway around the block.

"This better be worth it," Sara said.

"It's always worth it. That's the whole point of a line this long." He glanced sideways at her. "You said jiggly pancakes, specifically. Very specific request."

*"I saw a video of cute giggly pancakes. They wobble. I need to witness the wobble in person and definitely try them."* Sara said this yesterday, while waiting for ramen with Itadori.

"Wait\! This is the exact place from that viral video I told Yuji about," Sara said, looking at the storefront in realisation.

Gojo smirked, crossing his arms. "So really, you should be thanking me."

She looked up at him, a reluctant smile breaking across her face. "You're unbelievable."

While they waited, the conversation drifted somewhere quieter than the food debate the night before.

"So," Gojo said. "Where's home, actually? Not the PhD version. The real one."

"Small town, south of Seoul. Quiet, kind of spiritual, actually \- a lot of old temples nearby, monasteries in the hills. My parents moved me to Seoul when I was a teenager, for better schools." She shrugged. "Undergrad at Yonsei. Civil engineering. Now I'm here for a PhD in water resources and environmental engineering." Sara said this, flexing her top university degree.

"Water resources." He tilted his head. "So, water specifically. Not just things that fall down."

"Rivers, groundwater, flood systems, contamination. Anything that moves and needs managing before it becomes a disaster." She considered. "You could say I like knowing what's underneath something before it becomes a problem."

"Useful skill. I mostly specialise in things that already are the problem, so we balance each other out."

She gave him a look \- half amused, half unconvinced- and laughed quietly despite herself.

"It wasn't meant to be funny." He grinned. "Seoul to Tokyo, though. Not as much of a culture shock as you thought?"

"Flat-hunting, honestly. I thought it'd take a day. It took three, and I still didn't win \- hence the guesthouse, hence the eventual dorm." She shook her head. "Humbling experience."

"Tokyo real estate has humbled better people than you." He said it warmly enough that it didn't feel like an insult. "Your turn to ask something, I assume, since you clearly have a list."

"Where are you from?" Sara asked.

"Tokyo. Born and raised." No hesitation on that one \- safe ground. "Which is probably why I take the food this seriously. Home-field advantage."

"And school? Yuji calls you 'sensei' like you're some senior faculty member. You don't exactly look the part."

"Short version?" Gojo tilted his head, speaking in the slightly dramatic tone he reserved for explaining simple things to young kids. "I handle special cases. Kids with... excessive energy and a talent for getting into trouble. Less 'professor,' more 'high-level babysitter.'" He watched her reaction beneath his blindfold.

"That still doesn't explain how someone who looks barely older than a student ends up running the place."

"Great genes," he said simply, leaving it at that.

Most people would have dropped it after his signature grin, but Sara just raised an eyebrow.

"That's a deflection with extra steps," she noted.

Gojo's smile widened. "Look at that. You're already learning how I work."

She let it go, mostly because their order arrived, three stacked discs of pancake wobbling dramatically on the plate, and every other thought left her head.

"Oh my god," she said, delighted. "It's actually wobbling."

"Told you."

The first bite pulled an actual glow of satisfaction across her face. Emboldened, Gojo tapped the side of the plate with his fork, making the entire fluffy stack wobble dramatically again. Nearby shoppers turned to look. Sara lowered her fork, covering her face with a hand to hide her laughter.

"You're causing a scene," she whispered, entirely delighted.

"I am the one who ordered the wobbling dessert," Gojo declared, completely unbothered. "And I stand by it completely."

"This place doesn't exactly require hiking shoes," she said afterwards, licking cream off her thumb. "So what's the actual plan tonight?" Sara asked while enjoying tasty pancakes

"We're heading to Mitaka. National Astronomical Observatory."

She raised an eyebrow. "Didn't expect astronomy on today's itinerary."

"Mars is close tonight. Figured you'd appreciate the view, PhD or not."

"I appreciate anything that doesn't involve paperwork right now. Lead the way."

By the time they reached Mitaka, the sky had gone properly dark, streetlights giving way to the quieter hush around the observatory grounds.

They were halfway up the stone path, surrounded by the quiet rustle of trees, when Sara's sneaker caught the jagged lip of an unlit stone step.

Her balance snapped. Weight shifting backwards, gravity pulled her down toward the hard stone.

Before she could even gasp, Gojo moved in an effortless, fluid blur. His hand reached out, his fingers snapped around her hand, halting her fall instantly. The sudden pull brought her dangerously close, leaving barely an inch of night air between them.

For a long second, neither of them moved. Sara could feel the solid warmth of his hand around hers and the steady rise and fall of his breath. Up close, under the dim moonlit path, the sheer height and presence of him felt overwhelming in a way she hadn't noticed on the crowded streets.

"Careful," he murmured, his tone dropping out of its usual theatrical register into something low and quiet.

"Thanks," Sara managed, the word catching slightly, very aware of exactly how little space was left between them. Her stutter was partly from the fall, but mostly from the proximity.

He held her for a beat longer than necessary before releasing his grip, letting his fingers brush her hand as he stepped back, and neither of them mentioned it later.

The observation deck was quieter than she expected, just a handful of people scattered around the telescopes, necks craned back.

Through the eyepiece, Mars sat there \- small, reddish, unmistakably real in a way that photos never quite managed \- and above it, faint but visible, the pale wash of the Milky Way stretched across the dark.

"It's beautiful," she said quietly.

He looked at her for a moment longer than the question really needed, the blindfold catching the same faint starlight as the sky above them, before finally turning his gaze up as well.

"Yeah," he said.

She didn't know why, but something about the way he'd said it \- and the half-second delay before he looked up \- stuck with her.

They stood there a while, saying nothing, the sky doing most of the talking for them.

It was Sara who noticed the time first, glancing at her phone and immediately swearing under her breath.

"Curfew," she said. "We need to go. Now."

"Right behind you, warden."

They ended up sprinting the last two blocks like university freshmen dodging a dorm warden.

Gojo effortlessly jogging beside her with his hands in his pockets, offering absurd "coaching advice" while Sara wheezed in her heavy coat.

"Faster, Ms. PhD\! The gate waits for no one\!"

"Shut up and run\!" she wheezed out, breathless laughter bubbling up despite her aching lungs.

She slipped through the wooden gate with exactly two minutes to spare.

"Made it," she said, breathless.

"Told you I'd get you home on time." He was barely winded, which felt deeply unfair.

"Goodnight, Sara."

"Goodnight, Gojo."

She disappeared inside just as the gate began to close behind her.
