# Part \- 3 The Sweetest Dreams

*(Chapters 10–15. Sara wakes the morning after her first dream, flustered and sure it was only a collage of shrines, lanterns and sweets. She tells herself she has no crush on anyone real. Then Gojo goes silent for two weeks, with no replies from him or from Yuji. Meanwhile she settles into Tokyo: her first zemi, Mei’s friendship and the dream returning in softer variations. When he finally answers, he says he was busy, and they plan to meet on Saturday. His side of the story shows what he knows about her, what he hides, and a curse he quietly destroys while she, unaware, reacts to it. The part ends on a sweet note and dinner with Yuji, with the sense that something warm is quietly building. Nothing is wrong yet.)*

## Chapter 10 \- Things I Didn't Write Down

(Sara's POV)

Sara lay in bed with her blanket still wrapped around her like a very sad burrito. The sky outside had that dull blue-grey of a morning that hadn't made up its mind. Mei kept snoring.

*It was a dream. Just a dream.*

She turned her right hand over in the dim light and pressed the palm flat against the cool bedsheet, as if that would prove there was nothing unusual about it. Then she got annoyed at herself for checking.

Around six, she gave up on sleep.

She washed her face with cold water, which helped, and made her bed, which didn't. As she did it, her brain kept running a quiet little audit of the past week.

Gojo knew a lot about her. That was the problem.

He knew she lost keys and tripped over step edges. He knew she took her coffee iced, her pancakes jiggly, and was a proud fan with lots of Pop music knowledge. He knew she was from a temple town south of Seoul, that she studied water, and that she'd once believed his blindfold was an eye infection. He had been present for all of it, usually with a snack.

Sara folded her blanket and tried the same audit in reverse.

She knew he liked kikufuku and hated losing at arcade games. She knew he stayed a step behind her on stairs without ever saying he was waiting, that he bought yaki-imo like it was a tax, and that he hummed IDOL when he thought nobody was listening. She knew he'd picked her hairpin in about ten seconds.

And then the list just... stopped.

*Where does he live? Tokyo. Somewhere in it. How old was he? What did he actually do, besides the "high-level babysitting" he'd mentioned? What was Yuji's school? Why the blindfold? Fashion, mostly. Yuji had said something about sorcerers on the first night, and nobody had mentioned it since.*

Eleven days of sunsets and desserts, and she couldn't have filled out a form about him.

She picked up her toothbrush and went to the bathroom.

The mirror over the sink was too bright. She was halfway through brushing when she looked up, saw her own hair in a disaster, and, out of nowhere, the thought arrived while foam dripped onto her wrist.

*Oh. I'm pretty.*

It wasn't her thought. It belonged to a pretty girl in a shop full of mirrors, tilting her head and smiling back. Sara remembered it in pieces: yellow flowers in a bun, a light blush, glossy lips. Then candied strawberries, a hand around hers...

*We're not doing this.*

She brushed harder.

\---

The dorm didn't serve meals. The ground-floor common room had a shared kitchen with two induction stoves, a microwave, and a fridge shelf labelled by room number. At that hour, it belonged entirely to her.

She made rice with a fried egg, poured coffee that was stronger than it needed to be, and opened the diary she'd carried down with her.

It was a plain blue notebook she'd owned since undergrad and had never kept up for more than a month. She wrote the date. Then she sat with her pen hovering.

*Saturday.*

She could write about the hangover, Mei's dumplings, or the hour-long metro ride. She wrote a few lines about the shrine, Tasogare, twilight, and how it had made her think of Sailor Moon and then, unexpectedly, of her grandmother's town.

*G. took me. Again.* She looked at that, crossed out "again," then wrote it back in, because crossing things out felt dramatic.

*A friend,* she wrote. Then: *A very annoying one.*

She stopped and wondered whether he'd put it the same way. *Friend? New acquaintance? Someone he takes out because she's entertaining?* She had no answer, so she ate some egg.

Then the dream. The edges were already going soft like sugar left out in the rain, and she didn't try to write all of it. Just what stuck.

*sunset. lanterns. kimono (cream/yellow). yellow flowers. mirror shop, moon on a hand mirror. candied strawberry. fireworks. hairpin. "Ms Kang."*

She did not write *his thumb on my palm*. She did not write *he said I looked beautiful*.

A diary was supposed to be honest, but she decided she was only taking notes. And the explanation was very reasonable: she'd climbed a shrine, eaten yaki-imo, and walked a lantern street. Add every festival scene from every anime she'd ever watched, and her brain had made a collage.

She was almost sure she believed that.

She closed the notebook.

\---

When she got back upstairs, a little before nine, Mei was sitting up in bed with her hair in three directions and her glasses on crooked.

"You're awake," Mei said. "Yesterday you slept until mid-afternoon, and today you're dressed and showered before nine. You're very contrasting, Sara."

"Hangover. Different categories."

"Mm."

Mei padded off to get ready. Sara sat at her desk with a paper on tidal flats she'd already read three times and didn't absorb a single line. When Mei came back, towel around her neck, she took one look at her.

"You look like someone who has Thoughts."

"I'm fine."

"You're staring at page four as if it owes you money." Mei sat down on her bed. "Are you seeing anyone? Back home, I mean. You never said."

"No."

"Nobody?"

"Nobody." Sara turned a page. "I had a very serious relationship with my thesis."

"How did that end?"

"With a grade and mild trauma."

Mei snorted. "I'm single too, tragically. I want a cute Japanese boy, preferably in glasses, reading manga in a café. Or a Korean one. Introduce me to your friends."

"I know about three people in Tokyo."

"Two of them are men."

"One is sixteen."

"The *other* one." Mei's grin was wicked. "Mr Eyemask Tour Guide."

"He's a friend of Yuji's," Sara said, a little too fast. "A tour guide, basically. He's not available as a dating prospect for anyone."

The words were out before she could stop them. Mei's eyebrows rose slowly, with great ceremony.

"I just mean he's *a lot*," Sara said. "I don't have a crush on anyone real."

"Okay. Who's your crush, then?"

"Jiminie. My angel."

"Of course." Mei pressed a hand to her heart. "Difficult question \- Favourite song from the Love Yourself albums."

"That's like choosing a favourite child."

"Choose."

"I'll choose 2 \- *Serendipity* for my Jiminie and *Tear*  for Rapline*.*"

"My 2 favs \- *Fake Love* and *Pied Piper*. I'll fight anyone." Mei was laughing, and the conversation had moved on. It didn't occur to Sara until much later that Mei hadn't dropped the subject at all. She had just let it go, the way a good friend does.

---

Sunday settled into something slow and easy. They ate konbini onigiri and leftover rice, and Mei asked her to translate a few lines from a Korean article for something she was working on, and in return explained what her PhD actually was.

"It's postwar Japanese design," she said, spinning in her chair. "How posters, packaging, and manga covers ended up counted as art. My advisor says I'm ‘too fond of pop culture to be taken seriously,' which is the best compliment I've ever received." She groaned happily. "I have a presentation Thursday, and I'm going to lose my mind."

"You'll be fine."

"You'll be here when I lose it?"

"Obviously."

In the afternoon they watched the new episode of the anime they'd both been following, sharing a blanket and a bag of seaweed crisps. Then came a Korean drama episode that made them yell at the screen. By evening they'd sorted out the week: Sara's seminar prep, Mei's presentation, laundry, groceries, and a vow to cook something that wasn't instant noodles.

Her phone buzzed while she was folding the last of her laundry.

**Gojo**: *Legs alive?*

She smiled before she could stop it.

**Sara:** *Barely. Filing a second complaint.*  
**Gojo:** *Denied. They did great.*  
**Gojo:** *Also, you said middle ground next time.*  
**Sara:** *You remembered.*  
**Gojo:** *I remember everything that could get me in trouble.*  
**Sara:** *So where's the middle?*  
**Gojo:** *Somewhere with dessert. That's all I've got.*  
**Sara:** *That's a terrible plan.*  
**Gojo:** *It's a flexible plan.*

She laughed quietly. Across the room, Mei very carefully did not look up from her laptop.

Her thumb hovered over the keyboard. *Weird thing. I had a dream about a festival. You were in it.*

She read it once, then added, *We held hands during the fireworks.*

The cursor blinked at the end of the sentence like a bomb ticking down. Sara let out a sharp breath and deleted the whole thing.

**Sara:** *I have lab work all week. Weekend?*  
**Gojo:** *Weekend. I'll find the dessert.*  
**Gojo:** *Go to sleep, Ms Kang. You look like someone who has Thoughts.*

She stared at the screen. She hadn't told him that.

*Of course not.* Mei had said it that morning. He was just guessing, the way he guessed everything, with that infuriating confidence.

**Sara:** *Goodnight, Gojo.*  
**Gojo:** *Sweet dreams.*

She turned the phone face-down beside the blue notebook and told herself her heart was behaving normally.

## Chapter 11 \- Somewhere in It

(Satoru's POV)

Satoru was awake before his alarm, which was suspicious in itself.

Grey Sunday light slid through the curtains. He lay with one arm behind his head and found the other hand held up in front of his face, fingers loosely curled, as if he'd been checking it for something.

A wrist. One quick, startled flutter of a pulse under his thumb.

He put the hand down.

His phone sat on the nightstand, face-up and silent. He looked at it anyway.

For years, that phone had rung for exactly one reason: someone needed something killed. Ijichi, Yaga, the old men upstairs with their polite voices and impossible requests. It buzzed, and he went, and somebody's night got a little less terrible. He'd never minded. He'd never thought of it as a sound that could mean anything else.

Then a girl had asked for his number on a street corner, and now it could buzz with a complaint about her legs. Nobody dying. Nothing to fix.

It was the only noise that phone made that he didn't have to answer. He kept catching himself wanting to.

He got up to find breakfast, whistling something he refused to identify.

\---

Jujutsu High was quiet on Sundays, which meant Yuji and Megumi were sparring in the yard and Nobara, thankfully, was out shopping.

"Gojo-sensei\!" Yuji dropped his guard and got immediately flattened by Megumi. "Ow. You're early."

"I'm always on time."

"You're never on time," Megumi said, stepping over him.

Gojo held up a white box. "Kikufuku."

Yuji sat up so fast he nearly hit his head. "Is that a bribe?"

"A reward. For existing."

"You're in a good mood," Megumi said, eyeing him. "That's never good."

"Rude. I'm radiant." Gojo set the box on the engawa and flipped it open. "Help yourselves."

Yuji took one, chewed, and then grinned with powdered sugar on his chin. "Sara texted me. She said you looked betrayed at the dorm desk."

"I *was* betrayed. By an entire building."

"She said you called it discrimination against handsome men."

"Accurate."

Megumi, who had stopped chewing, looked up. "Who's Sara?"

"The girl from the street," Yuji said. "She's a friend of mine. From online."

"A tourist I'm guiding," Gojo said, with great dignity.

"Sensei's tourist," Megumi repeated slowly, and took a second kikufuku. "You don't guide anyone. You barely guide us."

Yuji coughed powdered sugar into his fist, shoulders shaking. Gojo clutched his chest in a tragic gesture and went on eating, because it was a Sunday and he had a box of kikufuku and, for once, nothing to prove.

\---

His phone rang a little after three, the old kind of ring, while he was lounging on the roof of the main building like a man with no responsibilities.

"Gojo-san," Ijichi said. "About tomorrow's assignment. It's the one out in the prefecture. I should warn you it may take a few days."

"How many?"

"Hard to say. Four, five days. It depends on how things develop."

"Can't it wait?"

"I'm afraid not, Gojo-san."

He sighed. "Fine. Send me the file."

He hung up and did the arithmetic. Four, five days. Monday through Friday. He'd be back by the weekend. He'd said weekend. She'd said weekend.

He thought, briefly, about texting her: 

*Busy this week, back by Saturday.* 

It seemed unnecessary. It was a few days. She had a lab and a roommate and a thesis, and he'd see her on Saturday as he'd said.

He thought no more about it.

\---

That evening he opened a chat with Sara and was almost embarrassed by how long he stayed there.

He typed three openers, each cleverer than the last, deleted all of them, and sent the dumbest one.

**Gojo**: *Legs alive?*

She replied within a minute. He grinned at the screen like it was personal.

No briefing. No casualty report. Just someone who'd been asked a stupid question and had answered it, and who, for the length of one conversation, knew him as a guy who liked kikufuku and lost at arcade games. He wasn't in a hurry to change that.

**Sara:** *Barely. Filing a second complaint.*  
**Gojo:** *Denied. They did great.*  
**Gojo:** *Also, you said middle ground next time.*  
**Sara:** *You remembered.*  
**Gojo:** *I remember everything that could get me in trouble.*  
**Sara:** *So where's the middle?*  
**Gojo:** *Somewhere with dessert. That's all I've got.*  
**Sara:** *That's a terrible plan.*  
**Gojo:** *It's a flexible plan.*

They were halfway through the argument when the replies stopped. A long gap. He watched the little dots appear, disappear, appear again, and then nothing, and then, entirely out of the blue:

**Sara:** *I have lab work all week. Weekend?*

Satoru tapped the phone against his chin.

Whatever she'd started to say, it hadn't been that. He knew the shape of an abandoned sentence. He didn't push. She'd never pushed him, not once, not about the neighbourhood or the blindfold or the high-level babysitting. It seemed only fair.

**Gojo:** *Weekend. I'll find the dessert.*

He thought of her at her desk, in that dorm room, chin in her hand, staring at something that wasn't her screen, and typed before he could stop himself.

**Gojo:** *Go to sleep, Ms Kang. You look like someone who has Thoughts.*

It was a guess. He was a very good guesser. Her *Goodnight, Gojo* came back a little too fast.

He typed the next two words, looked at them for longer than they deserved, and sent them anyway.

**Gojo:** *Sweet dreams.*

Then, because he'd just promised a girl a dessert and had no idea where the middle was, he opened another chat.

**Gojo:** *Itadori. Where's the middle between Shirokane and Hongo?*  
**Yuji:** *???*  
**Yuji:** *You don't know??*  
**Gojo:** *I know. I'm testing you.*  
**Yuji:** *Is it for Sara? Hmm....*  
**Yuji:** *She takes the Namboku line, right? Iidabashi. Kagurazaka has tons of sweet places*  
**Gojo:** *That was my plan.*  
**Yuji:** *Sure it was*

It was part of the job. Any decent guide would have done the same.

He put the phone down on his chest and stared at the ceiling.

Saturday, then. Something sweet, somewhere halfway, a girl who tripped over stair edges and ate like she was taking notes.

For once, he was looking forward to a weekend.

## Chapter 12 \- Exactly as Promised

(Sara's POV)

Monday went exactly the way Mondays were supposed to go.

The Namboku Line was packed, the lab smelled like coffee and old paper, and Sara spent the morning on a paper about tidal flats she had, for once, actually absorbed. By noon she had a plan for the week, a list of figures to rebuild, and absolutely no messages.

That was fine. Friends didn't text every day. She texted her parents maybe twice a month.

She checked her phone once, around three, purely to see the time. Then she slid it under a stack of printouts, where it couldn't embarrass her.

That night she fell asleep over her figures with the desk lamp still on.

\---

*Wait. I didn't fall?*

The lane tilts, her foot finds nothing, and then a hand closes around hers, warm and sure, and pulls her upright like she weighs nothing at all.

The crowd is still surging, but it isn't surging at them anymore. It's surging *after* them.

*Why is everyone chasing us?\!*

"Run, Ms Kang\!"

Gojo is laughing. He is actually laughing, head tipped back, white hair catching the lantern light, running with her hand in his like this is the best idea anyone has ever had. Behind them a wave of paper lanterns and festival masks bobs down the lane, a hundred shouting strangers, none of them looking remotely dangerous and all of them somehow terrifying.

*I'm in a kimono\! I can't run in a kimono\!*

She runs anyway, sandals slapping the stones, tassels swinging from her wrist.

"Left\!" he calls, and yanks her sideways behind a stall heaped with candied fruit, sugar shells glittering like jewels. He pulls her down beside him, one finger pressed to his lips.

The crowd thunders past. Someone shouts, someone else answers, and then the noise rolls away down the lane like a tide going out.

Sara's heart is hammering. Gojo's shoulders are shaking with silent laughter, and when she glares at him he starts humming, low and slow, a tune she has never heard before. It's strangely calming. Her breathing slows to match it.

*What song is that?*

"Hungry?" he whispers, and presses a paper bag into her hands. Warm castella bites, golden and soft, still faintly steaming.

She eats one. It's very good.

They sit behind the stall like two kids who've stolen something, passing the bag back and forth, and when the coast is finally clear they wander out into the lane again. The lanterns are lower now. The night smells of roasted chestnuts.

"You have crumbs," Gojo says.

"I do not."

"Right here."

His thumb rubs a smear of sugar and crumbs across her cheek, in a gesture that is not remotely helpful.

"You made it worse\!"

"Did I?" He sounds so pleased with himself. "Hold still, Ms Kang."

*Oh no. This is nice.*

\---

On Tuesday morning she woke up smiling. It took a full minute for her brain to catch up and find that embarrassing.

The tune stayed with her through the commute. She hummed a few bars under her breath, then into a music-recognition app: no BTS, no anime opening, no lullaby it could find. Nothing.

*I made it up*, she decided. *My brain is a very creative place.*

She was almost sure that was true.

\---

The week passed in the ordinary way weeks do.

On Thursday, Mei survived her presentation, and they celebrated with a convenience-store cake and a bottle of sparkling juice shared out of two coffee mugs. "You were here when I lost my mind," Mei said solemnly. "I'll remember that." Sara spent the rest of the evening on her own slides.

On Friday morning she sent a message before she could think about it.

**Sara:** *Presenting today. Wish me luck?*

It sat there. No reply. No "Read" mark either. She told herself he was just asleep somewhere, or in a meeting, or being eaten by a vending machine, and put the phone away.

\---

Her first zemi presentation was five minutes long, and by the end of it she had never been so aware of her own hands.

Five slides. Five minutes. Her whole PhD, boiled down to a handful of bullet points and one diagram of a river mouth she'd redrawn four times. The lab sat in a loose half-circle, laptops open, senior students in the front rows, Professor Hayashi quiet at the back with his arms folded.

"I'm interested in what happens after the water leaves," Sara said, and was relieved that her voice came out steady. "Most models treat the bed as fixed before an event. But repeated surges cut deeper during the retreat, and that part is poorly quantified."

She clicked to the last slide. "That's the gap I want to study."

Silence. Then a second-year doctoral student in the third row raised a hand.

"Why drawdown?" he asked, in English, politely. "Why not the inundation phase? It's better documented."

*I know this.*

She *did* know this. She'd written a whole paragraph about it in her proposal.

"Because the inundation phase is already well documented," she said, "and that leaves a gap. The retreat phase can put substantial shear stress on the bed and strip sediment as the water recedes, but most data sets stop recording once the water starts to go back."

Her voice shook slightly on the last word. She hated that.

The student nodded slowly. "That's reasonable," he said, and wrote something down.

From the back of the room, Hayashi said just one word. "Good."

That was all. Sara sat down with her hands still trembling and the strange, slightly ridiculous feeling that she had answered correctly and had still somehow managed to doubt it.

After the session, Hayashi stopped by her desk. "The lab monitors a river mouth on the Tohoku coast," he said, in the same mild tone he used for everything. "If the typhoons cooperate this season, I'd like you on the resurvey team. You will be going there regularly"

"Yes. Of course. Thank you."

He nodded and was gone before she had time to say anything more intelligent.

She said no to the lab dinner afterward. She said it politely, citing exhaustion, and everyone nodded as if exhaustion were a legitimate excuse. It mostly was.

\---

On Saturday, the weekend arrived exactly as promised, and nothing else did.

She didn't wait around for him. She did laundry, finished her figures, and went for a long walk around the neighborhood with her earphones in. But the phone stayed in her pocket the whole time, and every few minutes she caught herself thinking about Kagurazaka, a place she'd never even been to, and a dessert nobody had named.

*They never set a time,* she reminded herself. *It was a loose plan.*

Back in the room, she sent a different message.

**Sara:** *Yuji, what's your sensei up to? Has he been eaten by a vending machine?*

Nothing. Not even the little bubble.

Mei glanced over from her laptop. "Who are you texting?"

"Yuji."

"About Mr Eyemask Tour Guide?"

"About a lost tour guide." Sara put the phone on the desk. "He's busy. Tour guides have lives."

"Mm," Mei said, in the voice of someone who had decided to be nice about it.

That night, Sara opened the blue notebook and wrote the date. *Saturday. Finished figures. Walked a lot. Ate ramen.* She looked at the page for a while.

She did not write *no messages.*

She closed the notebook, turned the phone face-down beside it, and told herself that was a perfectly normal way to end a day.

## Chapter 13 \- Iced, No Sugar

(Sara's POV)

*We're still walking?*

The lane unrolls ahead of them, lantern after lantern, each one a little dimmer than the last. Her fingers are still tangled in his, and neither of them has mentioned it.

The crowd has thinned to a few couples and a cat on a windowsill. The stalls are drawing their curtains. Somewhere ahead, water is talking quietly to itself.

*A river. There's a river in this dream. Of course there is.*

They cross a small wooden bridge. At the far end a tea stall glows under a single paper lantern, its keeper nodding over a kettle. Gojo steers her to the bench and holds up two fingers without a word.

Two cups arrive, tall, beaded with cold, ice clinking.

"Iced," Gojo says. "No sugar. I remember."

Sara takes a sip. Roasted, bitter, wonderful.

*That's my Americano. Wearing a barley tea costume.*

Gojo has started humming again, that slow tune, tapping time against his knee. This time something in it catches, a familiar shape she can't quite place.

*I know this. Where do I know this from?*

Then it slips away, like a name on the tip of the tongue.

"Sorry to keep you waiting," he says quietly. It isn't a tease. His thumb finds the beads of her hairpin and sets them swinging.

*I wasn't waiting,* she thinks. *I wasn't.*

The water hushes. The lantern sways. And the dream goes on being the nicest place she has ever been.

\---

Sara woke on Sunday with her face in the pillow and her ears burning.

*Sorry to keep you waiting.*

She hadn't been waiting. Her brain had apparently written dialogue for him and put an apology in it, which was presumptuous of her brain.

She got up and went to find breakfast, since Mei was already gone from her bed.

\---

Mei was in the common-room kitchen, standing over two pots on the induction stoves with the focus of a chemist.

"Sit," she said. "Today you eat like a Singaporean."

She had smuggled a jar of Kaya in her suitcase, a green-gold coconut jam that she spread thick on toast with a slab of cold butter. Then came the eggs: soft-boiled, cracked into a saucer, with soy sauce and a dusting of white pepper.

"You dip the toast," Mei explained. "No, not like that. Like this."

Sara dipped. Chewed. Stopped.

"Okay," she said. "This is a crime. And I love it."

"I know." Mei was smug. "My grandmother would be proud."

It was a very good morning. They ate too much, and talked about nothing, and Sara checked her phone twice without noticing she was doing it. Mei watched the second time. She didn't say anything. She just pushed the last piece of toast across the table.

\---

On Tuesday night, a little after nine, her phone buzzed in the lab.

She was alone at her desk, rebuilding a figure for the fourth time. She almost didn't look.

**Gojo:** *Sorry I've been quiet. Work's eating me alive. I haven't forgotten the dessert. Hope your week's kinder than mine.*  
**Gojo:** *Do not replace me with another tour guide.*

Sara read it once. Then twice. Then a third time, slowly, as though it might change.

She wasn't angry. That was the strange part. She'd half-expected to be, and instead there was only a loosening somewhere under her ribs, the feeling of a window opening in a room she hadn't noticed was stuffy.

She typed, deleted, typed again.

**Sara:** *Applications are open. Also, do tour guides get overtime? Take care of yourself.*

She watched the screen for a full minute. No reply.

*Fine,* she thought. *He's busy.* It was enough that it had come at all.

Still nothing from Yuji. She stared at the empty thread, then shrugged. *Whatever Yuji's mysterious school does when it's busy, it clearly does it to everyone.*

She went back to her figure and finished it in half the time.

\---

On Wednesday, she was drying a mug in the common room when the weather forecast came on the old TV in the corner.

A smiling presenter pointed at a white swirl far out in the Pacific. "...a typhoon developing to the southeast. It may approach the eastern coast later this month, though the track is still uncertain."

Sara paused with the towel in her hand.

*A typhoon. Eastern coast.* She thought of Hayashi's river mouth, the resurvey, the *if the typhoons cooperate.*

Then she shrugged. Storms changed course all the time. Her own models were proof of that.

She hung up the mug and went to bed.

\---

*Is it raining?*

They're back on the bench at the tea stall, cups half-empty, when the first drop lands on the lantern with a soft *tap.*

*Wait. Am I in this dream again?*

Then another. Then a hundred.

"Run," Gojo says, grinning, and pulls her up.

They race back across the bridge, her sandals slapping the wet boards, laughter spilling out of her before she can stop it. He's holding his sleeve over her head like an umbrella, which is useless and ridiculous and she loves it. They duck beneath the deep eaves of a shuttered sweets shop, breathless, shoulders touching.

The rain drums on the roof. The lanterns blur to gold smears on the wet stones. Her hair is dripping, the hairpin is crooked, and he is laughing so hard that he has to lean a hand against the wall.

*His hair is wet. That is not fair.*

They stand there, close, listening to the water.

"I like this," Gojo says, when the laughter finally settles. He isn't looking at the street. He's looking at her, and his hand finds hers, warm and sure. "Standing somewhere I don't have to be anywhere else."

*Oh.*

*Oh, what is this weather, and this feeling? It makes me want to stay here.*

\---

She woke with her palm warm again.

She pressed it flat against the sheet. It did not help.

\---

On Friday evening, her phone lit up on her desk, and she nearly dropped it.

**Gojo:** *Back. Mostly in one piece.*  
**Gojo:** *Saturday?*

Sara stared. *Back.* Just like that, after almost two weeks.

She didn't ask where he'd been. She noticed that she didn't ask.

She typed a very cool reply, something about being generously free on Saturday, and deleted all of it.

**Sara:** *Okay.*  
**Gojo:** *Kagurazaka. Exact spot tomorrow morning.*  
**Sara:** *Why morning?*  
**Gojo:** *Suspense. A good guide has a sense of theatre.*  
**Sara:** *You're impossible.*  
**Gojo:** *Thank you.*

She put the phone down very carefully, as if it might go off.

Across the room, Mei looked up from her laptop. "Finally," she said, and went back to typing.

"I didn't say anything."

"You didn't have to."

Sara opened her desk drawer. The dark wood hairpin lay where she'd left it, beads curled on their thin chain, and for a moment she held it without moving.

*It's just a hairpin,* she told herself.

She set it on the desk, next to the blue notebook, where she would see it in the morning. And she did not think about what that meant.
