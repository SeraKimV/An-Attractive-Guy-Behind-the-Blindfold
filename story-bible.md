Primary:

* Romance  
* Supernatural mystery

Secondary:

* Slow burn  
* Fluff  
* Comedy

* Fantasy/supernatural  
* Eventually darker supernatural/psychological tension

The story should **feel romance-first initially**, with the supernatural mystery gradually becoming more prominent.

The goal is for readers to initially think:

> “Cute Gojo romance.”

Then gradually realize:

> “Wait... something is seriously wrong here.”

The supernatural mystery should **complicate the romance, not replace it**.

# Central Theme Idea

The main emotional theme developed for the story is:

> **Everyone sees Satoru Gojo, the Strongest. Sara slowly sees Satoru.**

Gojo is used to people seeing:

* the strongest sorcerer  
* the Six Eyes  
* someone terrifying/untouchable  
* an extremely powerful teacher  
* an attractive man  
* someone people fear or admire

Sara, however, instinctively experiences him differently.

She eventually tells him that she finds him **reliable** and that he gives her a strange sense of **warmth**, despite being a stranger.

This affects Gojo deeply because people don't normally describe him as warm or safe.

The reverse theme is also important:

Everyone assumes Sara is an ordinary Korean PhD student, but Gojo eventually discovers there is something unusual about her that **even Sara doesn't know about herself**.

# Sara’s “Warmth” Line

In the original chat, Sara tells Gojo something along the lines of:

> she finds him reliable somehow  
> he gives off warmth  
> she doesn't know why  
> she normally hasn't trusted that feeling from a stranger

This is a key emotional line.

However, in the revised novel version, we decided **not to make it sound like Sara has deeply understood Gojo after five minutes.**

Instead, it should feel like the statement **slips out of her unexpectedly**, even surprising Sara herself.

For example:

> “I don't know why, but...”

> She hesitated.

> “You seem reliable.”

> Gojo blinked.

> “Reliable?”

> “Yeah. You give off this... warmth. It's strange. I trust you, and I don't even know you.”

This makes the line emotionally powerful without making the relationship advance unrealistically fast.

# Dream Mystery (REVISED)

## Core logic · DECIDED

* Sara's early dreams are **sweet and whimsical**. They read as a crush dream: a flustered, believable reaction to a guy she has known for about a week.
* **A quiet hidden cause sits underneath** (her childhood "receiver" residue; see **Sara's Hidden Ability**). Neither Sara, Mei nor the reader needs to suspect it at first.
* The dreams **do not become unsettling on their own.** They turn only after Sara touches the object at the start of Part IV (Ch 16). Until then, no voice and no "continue".
* When the object arrives, it does not invent a new channel. It **hijacks the sweet dream that already exists** and turns it against Gojo. Because dream-Gojo has been a safe place, the betrayal hurts more.
* **Plant one or two dream details that are not Sara's.** Only in hindsight should the reader realise some of the "whimsical" dream was never hers.

## Dream 1 — written in Ch 9 · DECIDED

Setting and look:
* An autumn dusk, an apricot-and-rose sky, a wooden shop lane strung with paper lanterns, roasted chestnuts on the air, everyone in kimono and yukata.
* Sara wears a cream-to-yellow kimono with a brown sash and small yellow flowers in her bun. Gojo wears a black and red yukata, loose over one shoulder. The blindfold is still on.
* She finds a mirror shop. She buys a small hand mirror with a crescent moon traced on its edge, then a candied strawberry from a stall. (Candied strawberries, not tanghulu.) Fireworks begin.

Beats that matter:
* Gojo's line is sincere, not a tease: "Looking beautiful, Ms Kang." Then: "You still have the hairpin I picked."
* They hold hands; his thumb moves across her palm. It carries the same warmth she felt on the street in Ch 1.
* A crowd surges, his hand slips away, she falls, and she wakes on the floor beside her bed.
* She is mortified that a man she has known for about a week appeared in her dream. Her palm is still faintly warm.

**The mirror shop stays completely innocent for now** (DECIDED). No uncanny detail in Part III. Its payoff comes after the object appears.

## How the dreams should run through Part III · DECIDED / PROPOSED

* **DECIDED:** the same dreamscape recurs, with variations, for roughly a week and on and off for the rest of the month. Sara goes from flustered to enjoying it and settling in.
* **DECIDED:** she is flustered when she meets Gojo after a dream, but she is too shy to tell him. The reader knows; Gojo does not.
* **DECIDED (Ch 10):** she writes the dream in a notebook or diary over breakfast and keeps analysing it, looking for a rational explanation.
* **PROPOSED variations** (same lane and same lanterns, a different moment each time): the shrine stairs at twilight, a riverside with fireworks, a quiet tea stall, a rainy version, a dream where Gojo has no blindfold but she cannot see his face.
* **PROPOSED details that are not Sara's** (pick one or two, plant them lightly):
  * a tune dream-Gojo hums that she has never heard and cannot identify when she tries to look it up
  * a stall-keeper who greets her by a name that is not hers
  * a lane or shop name written in an old form of the kanji that she can read but should not know
* **PROPOSED:** dream-Gojo is slightly gentler and less teasing than waking Gojo. Part of the reason Sara is flustered is that she likes him better there.

## When Sara finally tells Gojo · DECIDED / PROPOSED

* **DECIDED:** she tells him in Part IV, when the dreams turn unsettling.
* **PROPOSED:** when she does, she also confesses the sweet dreams. Gojo reacts the way the original plan describes: he jokes about them, while privately noticing that some details match things he knows.

# Sara's Hidden Ability (REVISED)

Sara has an unexplained sensitivity to **concentrated, high-energy supernatural presences**.

She does NOT suddenly become a conventional jujutsu sorcerer. She cannot simply see every curse or start fighting like a trained sorcerer.

She can:

* sense unusually strong energy
* instinctively notice people like Gojo and Yuji
* be drawn toward high-energy supernatural presences
* eventually sense the cursed object

She does not understand that this is an ability.

## Where it comes from: the "receiver" · DECIDED

Sara is a **receiver**, not a sorcerer. She has very little cursed energy of her own, only a faint, permanent resonance, a residue left by a childhood encounter with the same mirror that later turns up on her fieldwork.

**What happened to her as a child (author's account, October 2026)**

1. A **heavy flood after heavy rain** in her hometown (the author first said earthquake, tsunami or flood, then settled on flood) uncovered the **buried mirror** at a flood-prone spot near the temple. This was the Korean half of the split mirror (see **Cursed Object**).
2. The flood **broke the jar and the mirror inside it.** Little Sara found the pieces, **touched them**, and **was affected by their power** (she fainted).
3. **The old monk** from the nearby temple **gathered the broken pieces**, **sealed them**, and **healed Sara**.
4. The old monk told her parents she had caught a **fever from getting wet in the rain**.
5. **Only the old monk who treated her knows** what she carries. Sara does not know, and neither do her parents.

**PROPOSED details**

* She was around five or six.
* She remembers only "a bad sickness" and a temple bell.
* **Disaster (DECIDED):** a flood, not an earthquake or tsunami. It suits an inland town south of Seoul and matches the "got wet in the rain" cover story.
* Her parents' move to Seoul "for better schools" (Ch 4) can quietly double as leaving that town. Nobody has to say so.
* The mirror half that survives in Japan **recognises the residue** the Korean half left in her. That is one reason it reacts to her so strongly. (See **Cursed Object**.)

## What the ability explains in chapters already written · DECIDED

* **Ch 1:** Gojo's cursed energy reacts to her before he understands why, and his blindfold lifts.
* **Ch 1:** she notices Gojo in a crowd and, unusually for her, trusts him on instinct.
* **Yuji:** the original plan lists "connects with Yuji" as part of the same pattern. Their friendship began online a year earlier, before the story, so the ability cannot be the cause. **OPEN:** treat it as coincidence, or let it become a later "wait, was that why?" moment.
* **Ch 5:** Infinity let her through. It treated her as cleared rather than as an unknown.
* **Ch 8:** her fingers brush the blindfold and he does not flinch.
* **The "warmth":** the thing she keeps calling warmth is partly her sensitivity reading him. That is why it feels trustworthy to her and slightly unnerves him. Do not say this outright until it is earned.

## What Gojo knows, and when · DECIDED

* From Chapter 1, Gojo knows **what** is different about her: an unusual sensitivity to energy.
* He does **not** know **why** until later. The temple link comes in Part IV, before the Korea trip. The full picture arrives on the trip in Part V, where the old monk fills in what happened to her as a child.

# Engineering / Fieldwork Arc (REVISED — formerly "Engineering / Excavation Arc")

Sara's actual PhD and research work takes her to a field site on the coast.

This is important because the supernatural plot should arise naturally from **her real life**. It is no longer a random excavation. Her own research question exposes the object.

Before this:

> The supernatural mystery is happening around Sara.

After this:

> **The supernatural has physically entered Sara's life.**

## Her research · DECIDED

**Working title:** *Quantifying Coastal Morphodynamics and Subsurface Sediment Transport in Multi-Event Inundation Zones.*

* Studies how repeated typhoon surges and tsunami inundation strip coastal sediment and topsoil, especially during the **retreat (drawdown) phase**.
* Fieldwork on Japan's Tohoku coast: ground-penetrating radar (GPR) surveys, soil core sampling, and post-storm or low-tide resurveys at estuaries.
* Supervisor: Professor Hayashi. The project and field site are refined through her first months of zemi and one-on-one meetings (see Ch 6). The sediment-transport interest she names to Hayashi in Ch 6 is the same one that leads her to the site.
* The topic was chosen deliberately so that a storm surge or an unusual tidal drawdown exposes something buried, so the water itself is the reason it surfaces. Full research notes are in `Sara_PhD_program_research.md`.

## How the discovery works

* **DECIDED:** it comes from her fieldwork, at the **start of Part IV (Ch 16)**, on a Monday, not from a staged excavation. It is found at a **flood-prone or tidal area after heavy flooding or rain**, uncovered by the water rather than dug up. Hayashi assigns the field survey (planted in Ch 12–13), and Sara finds the broken vessel and the mirror during it. Light from the mirror hits her eyes and she faints. Her team calls it an old artifact and site work is halted as a possible heritage find.
* **OPEN, deferred by the author:** the exact field site. The author will decide and check it when that chapter is reached. Her thesis stays "Tohoku coast". One idea, not a decision: the Miyagi coast / Sendai Plain, where sand deposits from the 869 AD Jogan tsunami are well documented in coastal-sediment research, which would put the object under a tsunami layer from Sukuna's own era (Heian). Check any geological detail before it goes on the page.
* **PROPOSED mechanism:** a late-season typhoon surge, heavy flooding or an unusually deep drawdown scours an estuary or river-mouth trench and exposes a **sealed porcelain jar** (the author's wording) with the Japanese half of the mirror inside. Her GPR can pick up the anomaly first. **Realism:** true porcelain was not made in Japan until the early 1600s, imports aside, so a stoneware or celadon-type jar would be more period-accurate for a Heian-era object. Decide before writing.
* Gojo is **not** there when she finds it.
* Sara handles it. It feels strangely warm. She assumes it is ordinary archaeology and tells no one it felt odd.

## Realism notes for the writing · to verify before publishing

* In Japan, an archaeological find is normally reported to the local board of education and the cultural-heritage authorities. **OPEN:** decide where the jar is held afterwards (a university lab or museum store while it waits for assessment) so that Sara stays within reach of it.
* This is a chance for the engineering side of Sara to show: she should treat the find as data to log, photograph and protect, not as a treasure.

# Cursed Object (REVISED — origin decided October 2026)

We decided it should preferably **not simply be another Sukuna finger**.

Instead:

> A relic/fragment/object connected to Sukuna.

It contains residual cursed energy associated with Sukuna.

It is capable of resonating with Sukuna because Sukuna is inside Yuji.

The supernatural connection becomes:

**Sara ↔ cursed object ↔ Yuji/Sukuna**

Sara can sense the object because of her hidden sensitivity.

The object reacts to Sara.

Sukuna eventually becomes aware of her through the connection.

## What it is · DECIDED

* A **mirror** that was a **medium to Sukuna**.
* It was **split into two parts**, and **each part was sealed inside a porcelain jar**.
* **Part one** is found during Sara's fieldwork at the start of Part IV, in Ch 16 (see **Engineering / Fieldwork Arc**).
* **Part two** was buried **below a temple in South Korea**, in Sara's hometown area.
* Not a finger, and not a cloth.

## What happened to Sara as a child · DECIDED

* A **heavy flood after heavy rain** uncovered the buried Korean mirror at a flood-prone spot near the temple, and **broke the porcelain jar and the mirror inside it.**
* Child Sara found the broken pieces, **touched them**, and **was affected by their power** (she fainted).
* **The old monk** from the temple/shrine gathered the broken pieces, **sealed them, and healed Sara.**
* The monks told her parents she had **a fever from getting wet in the rain.**

# Important Dialogue Refinement Instruction

The original AI chat sometimes makes Gojo and Sara jump too quickly into emotionally intimate or overly polished dialogue.
