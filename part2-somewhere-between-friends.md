# Chapter 5 \- Brunch, Banter, and a Question He Dodged

(Satoru's POV)

The walk home took longer than it should have.

Not because Tokyo had suddenly grown wider. Satoru just wasn't in a hurry.

His hands were in his pockets, his blindfold back where it belonged, and his mind was doing something it rarely bothered with: replaying it. Not the observatory, not Mars, not even the sprint back to her gate with two minutes to spare. Just her. The small, specific things.

Sara Kang.

He frowned slightly.

That was new.

He'd met plenty of people over the years. But Sara had somehow stuck in his head after only a few meetings.

And it wasn't even because of anything dramatic.

It was the little things.

The look on her face had cycled through three different things in under a second when he'd introduced himself \- skeptical, then amused, then flat-out laughing \- like she genuinely couldn't tell whether "strongest sorcerer alive" was a real title or a very confident pickup line.

Then she'd chosen a library over him.

Then she'd asked for his number like it hadn't occurred to her that he might say no.

Then she'd called him warm.

Warm. Not powerful. Not terrifying. Not the strongest. Warm, like he was a blanket, or bad coffee, or something a person could get used to.

Satoru huffed a laugh at himself, alone on an empty street, which was probably a bad sign.

The observatory moment was worse. He kept returning to it \- not the catch itself, a foot of movement and half a heartbeat \- but what came right before it. The half-second between her ankle rolling and his hand closing around hers.

He hadn't dropped Infinity.

He'd just \- let her through.

That part bothered him more than it should have.

Since when does Infinity make exceptions for strangers?

It wasn't a decision he remembered making. His body had simply decided, faster than thought, that she was allowed. Not filtered as debris, not treated as an unknown variable to be calculated around. Allowed. Like she already had clearance he hadn't consciously issued.

That should have bothered him. Filed under: things to think about later, not tonight.

His phone was already in his hand by the time he reached his door.

Gojo: You survived the great Tokyo tourist experience.

The reply came faster than he expected.

Sara: Barely. My legs are still filing a complaint.  
Gojo: Those shoes performed excellently. I take full credit.  
Sara: You take credit for everything.  
Gojo: Only the good stuff.

He grinned.

Gojo: And also you have terrible balance.  
Sara: Excuse me?  
Gojo: I saved your life.  
Sara: I tripped.  
Gojo: Exactly. A life-threatening situation.  
Sara: You're unbelievable.  
Gojo: Thank you.

A pause.

Sara: Goodnight, Gojo.

Satoru stared at the message for a second.

Gojo: Wait.  
Sara: What?  
Gojo: Apparently, there's a place that opened up near Shimokitazawa. brunch. I've heard good things.  
Sara: Apparently?  
Gojo: I haven't tried it yet.  
Sara: So you're using me as a test subject?  
Gojo: No. I'm rescuing you from guesthouse breakfast.  
Sara: I haven't even told you what I eat for breakfast.  
Gojo: You're welcome.  
Gojo: 11\. Don't be late. I won't wait for you; I'll just eat your portion.  
Sara: You wouldn't.  
Gojo: Try me, Ms Kang.  
Sara: See you at 11\. Don't order without me. Goodnight, Gojo.  
Gojo: See you tomorrow, Ms Tourist. Good Night\!

He set the phone down on the counter, looked at it for a beat longer than the message warranted, and then made himself stop looking at it.

Sara Kang. He still didn't have the rest of that thought yet. He went to bed without finding it.

The restaurant was exactly as new as advertised \- pale wood, hanging plants, a menu that took itself a little too seriously for a Sunday morning. Satoru had already been seated by the window for a few minutes when he spotted her down the street, jogging, dodging two separate pedestrians, phone in one hand and the strap of her bag sliding off her shoulder with the other. She nearly hit her head on the edge of a signpost near the crosswalk hard enough that he winced on her behalf, then course-corrected without breaking stride.

Satoru laughed.

By the time she pushed through the door, hair slightly damp, she was four minutes late anyway.

"Did you already order my portion?" she said, sliding into the seat across from him.

"Good morning, Ms Tourist. I would have ordered if you showed up a minute late. Ruthlessly."

The menu had everything from fluffy French toast and thick-cut toast to omelettes, fruit plates and Japanese-style brunch sets.

"I've picked my choice \- French toast", Satoru said, already scanning the rest of the menu.

"That's not a meal."

"You sound like Itadori."

She smiled and said, "I'll get pancakes and an iced Americano then."

"Iced Americano?"

"You won't find a Korean who dislikes coffee. It's practically a personality requirement."

He looked at her choices, unimpressed. She pointed right back at his.

"We're ordering two desserts for breakfast."

"Brunch."

"Whatever."

They ordered the chocolate pancakes, the French toast, and her coffee. And the food, when it came, was worth whatever hype had gotten it onto his phone in the first place \- golden French toast dusted with sugar, pancakes dripping chocolate at the edges, the iconic Iced Americano, and a complimentary fruit plate disappearing faster than either of them would admit to.

Sara took one bite of his toast, then a sip of her coffee, and her eyes widened.

"Oh."

Satoru grinned. "Good?"

"Okay," she admitted, around a bite she was clearly enjoying more than she wanted to say out loud. "This was a good call."

"See? I'm a genius."

"You didn't cook it."

"Still my recommendation."

She shook her head, smiling, and tapped her fork against her plate before taking another bite \- savouring it, methodical about it, like she was taking notes.

"You're analyzing the food," he said.

"I'm appreciating it."

"That's a very academic way of eating."

"I'm an engineer."

"Same thing."

She pointed her fork at him. "Don't insult engineers."

"I wouldn't dare."

They both laughed.

Some pop song was playing low over the restaurant speakers, and Sara hummed along without seeming to notice she was doing it.

"You're into that?" Satoru asked.

"Very." No hesitation, no self-consciousness \- just a fact. "I'm a huge Bangtan Sonyeondan fan. J-pop too, and a few Korean groups on top of that. I actually learned Japanese because of anime and music, way before the PhD was ever a plan."

That he had not expected. "I had you filed under 'probably listens to Chopin or Beethoven.'"

She stared at him skeptically. "Why?"

"An engineering PhD student into K-pop? I'm devastated. My worldview is in ruins." He leaned back, arms crossed, delighted. "Go on, then. Enlighten me."

She did \- group names, comebacks, a very strong opinion about one particular choreography he had zero context for. He let most of it wash past him, mostly watching her talk rather than tracking the content, until she paused for breath and gave him a pointed look.

"You're not even listening."

"I'm absolutely listening. I retained maybe 40%."

"...40%?"

"I'm Tokyo-born. This stuff has been playing in every convenience store and taxi I've ever been in since I was seven. I know the choruses. I don't know the group names attached to them."

"That's not the same as liking it."

"Never said I liked it. Said I couldn't escape it." He smiled. "You, on the other hand. Genuinely enthusiastic. It's a good look on you."

She rolled her eyes, smiling, and it took her a second longer than usual to look back down at her plate.

"Okay. How much do you actually know?"

"Enough to win."

"You don't even know the choreography."

"I don't need choreography."

"That's exactly what someone says right before embarrassing themself."

Satoru leaned back. "Are you challenging me?"

Sara smiled. "Maybe."

He pointed toward the door. "Arcade, after this? A dance battle?"

"Sure. Let's find out who's the best."

It was somewhere between the last of the pancakes and the last of the fruit plate that Sara tilted her head at him, studying the blindfold like she was noticing it properly for the first time all over again, and then stopped, distracted by something else entirely.

"You have chocolate sauce on your cheek."

"Is there?" Satoru didn't reach for a napkin. "Good. I'm enjoying it."

She laughed, shook her head, and let it go \- then, a beat later:

"By the way, can I ask you something?" Sara asked, leaning her chin on her hand.

"Depends on what it costs me," Satoru replied, grinning.

"When I first saw you," she said, "before I knew anything \- I genuinely thought you might have an eye infection. Like, a bad one. I thought the blindfold was temporary."

Satoru laughed \- a real one, loud enough that a couple at the next table glanced over. "An eye infection."

"You looked very serious about it\! I thought you were being brave."

"I am very brave," he said. "Just not about conjunctivitis." He tapped the fabric lightly with one finger. "It's permanent. Fashion, mostly. Also insurance for people who can't handle this face at full wattage."

"That's not an answer."

"It's the answer you're getting."

She studied him a second longer than the joke really called for, and something in him noted it \- quietly, underneath the grin he kept perfectly in place. Nobody asked like that. Most people already knew, or didn't dare ask at all, or asked in a way that was really asking about the Six Eyes, about power, about the myth. She'd asked like it was just a piece of him she was curious about. Nothing underneath it. Nothing she wanted from the answer except the answer.

He didn't let any of that reach his face. "Careful, Ms Kang. Keep asking questions like that, and I'll start thinking you actually care."

"Deflection," she said, pointing her fork at him again. "Noted."

She laughed and let it go, and he was grateful for that in a way he didn't examine too closely.

"Tomorrow's the real start, by the way," she said, a little while later, stirring the last of her coffee. "Classes. Orientation stuff. Actual campus life instead of enrollment forms." She exhaled. "New course, new city, new everything. Feels like it's actually starting now."

Somewhere outside the window, Tokyo carried on with its ordinary Sunday in no particular hurry to announce itself, students everywhere quietly bracing for the same reset she was.

"You'll survive," Satoru said. "Probably. I've seen your track record with keys and step edges, so I'm not promising anything."

"Thank you for the confidence."

"I'm your guide, not your good luck charm."

"Could you be both?"

"I contain multitudes," he said, and she laughed, and that settled something loose and warm in his chest that he decided not to think about until later.

The arcade was louder than Satoru remembered. Machines flashed around them, music overlapping from every direction. Sara stopped when she saw the dance rhythm game.

"You actually brought me here."

"I said arcade."

She stepped onto the platform beside him.

"One round."

The music started.

For the first few seconds, Satoru was completely confident. Then Sara started moving.

He blinked. \- She was good \- definitely much better than someone who had claimed she was only a casual fan.

"You lied to me," he said between movements.

"I never said I couldn't dance."

"You implied it."

"I absolutely did not."

The song sped up. Sara missed one step and laughed. Satoru grinned, only to miss the next one himself.

The next round was worse \- they were both trying far too hard. At some point, Satoru stopped thinking about winning. He was just having fun. Real fun. The kind that didn't require him to be the strongest person in the room. He didn't have to be Gojo-sensei. He didn't have to think about curses or missions or students.

He could just be Satoru.

Sara missed a step and nearly stumbled off the platform. His hand reached out automatically, and she caught his arm.

"Again?" she asked.

He laughed. "Apparently your balance still needs work."

"Maybe you're just bad luck."

"Impossible."

She let go of his arm, still laughing. "One more round."

"Now you're addicted."

"You're the one who brought me here."

"Fair point."

They played until the afternoon light began fading through the arcade windows.

Eventually, Sara checked her phone. "Oh. It's almost six."

"Campus life starts tomorrow?"

She nodded. "Orientation, lectures, paperwork... probably more paperwork."

"Sounds thrilling."

"Very." She picked up her bag. "I should get back early and organise everything."

"Responsible."

"Unlike someone."

"Hey."

She smiled. "Thank you for today."

He paused. The words were simple. But they landed differently. "Anytime."

He walked her back with the sun still up, early enough that the guesthouse gate closing behind her felt like nothing more than the end of an ordinary afternoon.

"Good luck tomorrow," he said. "Try not to lose anything important on your first day."

"I'm not that clumsy\! And don't jinx me\!" she laughed.

"I'll see you around?"

"Goodnight, Gojo." She gave a warm wave before turning and disappearing through the gate.

Satoru started walking home, hands in his pockets, his mind returning to the same three things: the eye infection theory, the choreography she shouldn't have known, and the small, easy way Infinity had let her through.

Sara Kang.

What are you, he thought, not for the first time, and not, he suspected, for the last.

# Chapter 6 \- No Visitors Past the Lobby

(Sara's POV)

Sara's first thought after lying down on her bed that night wasn't about the research waiting for her the next morning. It wasn't about her new university, her advisor, or the stack of papers she'd promised herself she would read before classes properly began.

It was about a terrible arcade dance game, a stack of pancakes, and a smear of chocolate sauce on the cheek of a man who had somehow gone from complete stranger to someone she had spent most of the weekend thinking about.

Two days ago, Gojo had been a stranger standing too close on a sidewalk, giving her an introduction so ridiculous she'd assumed he was joking. Now she knew what his laugh sounded like. She knew he liked kikufuku, was annoyingly competitive at arcade games, and, apparently, was capable of catching her before she hit the ground.

This is moving fast.

Sara stared at the ceiling. She wasn't sure whether that was a warning or simply an observation. Either way, she was too tired to investigate it tonight. Tomorrow was her first proper day at the university, and she had enough things to worry about.

She closed her eyes.

The Department of Civil Engineering didn't look anything like the university orientation she'd half-braced herself for.

There was no huge auditorium. No welcome speech. No row of people handing out identical folders and telling everyone to smile for photographs.

Instead, she was handed a building map, pointed down a corridor, and told something about the laboratory so quickly that Sara only caught the last few words.

"Sorry?"

The woman repeated herself.

Sara nodded. "Ah. Yes. Thank you." She had absolutely no idea what she'd just agreed to.

Five minutes later, she was standing outside a laboratory on one of the upper floors, staring at a small sign beside the door. Her academic home, apparently.

Inside, six other graduate students were already working. Computers lined the desks, and papers were stacked beside monitors, under keyboards, and literally everywhere else. Her assigned desk was wedged comfortably between two senior students.

One of them introduced himself, showed her where to find the shared equipment, and explained which shelves she could use. Then came her supervisor.

Professor Hayashi was a quiet man in his fifties who shook her hand once, asked three sharp questions about her master's research, and somehow managed to make her feel like she'd been sitting for an examination without ever raising his voice.

"What do you want to continue working on?"

Sara paused. She had expected the question, but she still hadn't expected it to feel so heavy.

"I'd like to stay in water resources and environmental engineering," she said. "I'm interested in flooding and coastal systems."

Hayashi nodded. "Specific area?"

Sara glanced down at the research proposal she'd brought with her. "I keep coming back to places where water doesn't just happen once."

He waited.

"Coastal areas. Places affected repeatedly by storm surges, typhoons, tsunamis..." She searched for the precise words. "I'm interested in what happens to the sediment when those events keep returning. Especially during inundation and when the water retreats."

Something in his expression sharpened. "Sediment transport."

"Yes."

"Good. We can discuss the exact direction after you've settled in. Your proposal is a good starting point. The field site and methodology can be refined."

Sara nodded. That made sense. She wasn't expecting anyone to hand her a finished PhD thesis on her first day.

By the end of the afternoon, Sara had a university ID, campus access, a pile of reading material, and approximately twelve new passwords she was expected to memorize.

Her PhD had officially begun \- and she was already exhausted.

By Tuesday afternoon, she had her first zemi \- a research seminar.

Sara sat quietly while a senior student presented flood-simulation results on a screen at the front of the room. The presentation moved quickly between Japanese and dense technical terminology, but she understood enough to follow the core argument.

And enough to know what she needed to look up later.

When the presentation ended, the room immediately filled with sharp discussion. Someone questioned a model assumption; someone else pointed out an inconsistency in the data.

Sara watched carefully. This wasn't about memorizing information. Everyone in the room was expected to challenge the research.

She liked that. It felt familiar. Difficult, but real.

By the time she returned to her temporary guesthouse that evening, she had decided two things: she was going to need a much better notebook, and significantly stronger coffee.

Wednesday was moving day.

Sara had barely finished packing the last of her things when someone knocked on the guesthouse door. She opened it to find Yuji standing outside with two empty cardboard boxes.

Gojo stood right behind him, hands in his pockets. Empty-handed.

Sara stared at him. "You're not holding anything."

"I'm supervising."

"That's not a real job."

"It's my favorite job."

Yuji laughed, handing over the spare boxes as Sara stepped aside to let them in.

"Sorry for dragging you into this on a busy day," Sara said, gathering her loose belongings. "And Yuji, I didn't expect you to pull your sensei into it too."

"I didn't pull him\!" Yuji said defensively. "He practically volunteered."

As Gojo stepped into the room, his eyes landed directly on an unsealed box containing anime figures, several loosely rolled K-pop posters, and a stack of manga. He went still for a second, almost impressed.

"I knew you had a collection," he said slowly. "I didn't realize it was an operation."

"It's organized."

"It's a shrine, Ms Kang. A dedicated, two-box shrine."

"Just leave it open," Sara said, cheeks warming slightly. "We'll carry them as they are and sort it out in my new room."

The residence was tucked into a quiet residential street in Shirokane \- an international women's dormitory with shared rooms, cozy common spaces, and strict rules about visitors.

When Sara unlocked her assigned room, a girl sitting cross-legged on one of the beds looked up from her laptop. She had short dark hair tucked behind one ear, oversized headphones around her neck, and an anime keychain dangling from her tote bag.

"Oh," the girl blinked. "You're Sara Kang?"

"Yeah."

The girl smiled immediately. "I'm Stefany Mei."

"Nice to meet you."

Sara unloaded the first round of boxes and headed back down for the rest.

The real disaster happened at the main entrance.

She'd gone down to collect the final box when she spotted Gojo standing at the security desk.

The security staff behind the counter looked completely unimpressed. Gojo, meanwhile, looked personally offended.

"I'm just helping her move her things," Gojo was saying, leaning casually against the desk.

"Residents only beyond the lobby," the security staff replied, not even looking up from her logbook.

"I'll be very quick. Two minutes."

"No."

Gojo flashed his signature, charming smile \- the one that usually got him whatever dessert or outcome he wanted.

The security staff didn't even blink. "Boxes can be handed over the counter."

Sara stopped at the edge of the hallway. Yuji was standing beside Gojo, clutching his stomach and trying very hard not to burst out laughing.

Gojo slowly looked at the heavy box in his arms, then at the counter, then over his shoulder at Sara.

"Apparently," he said loudly, "I've been demoted to a delivery service."

Sara covered her mouth to smother a laugh. "Gojo-san..."

"This is discrimination."

"Against men?" Sara asked.

"Against handsome men."

Yuji completely lost it, leaning against the wall, wheezing. "Gojo-sensei, just give her the box\!"

"You're supposed to be my student, Yuji. Support me."

"I support the security staff," Yuji choked out.

"Traitor."

The security staff cleared her throat with lethal precision. "Counter. Now."

Gojo sighed dramatically and slid the box onto the desk. Sara stepped forward and pulled it to her side.

"Thank you for the delivery," she teased.

"You're very welcome," Gojo replied, crossing his arms. "Do you want me to stay down here and supervise the lobby?"

"No."

"Harsh."

"You're banned from the upper floors."

"I noticed."

Sara hauled the box up herself, climbing the stairs with her arms full while, somewhere below, her two self-appointed movers remained exactly as useless as they'd been since arriving. "Some help you two turned out to be," she muttered, mostly to herself, mostly fondly.

When she finally pushed the door open with her hip, arms full and slightly out of breath, Mei looked up from the growing pile already on the floor. "Need help?"

"That would actually be great."

Within ten minutes, Mei was helping her organize. They quickly discovered they were both PhD students \- Mei was doing hers in modern arts. Then Mei noticed the rolled-up posters sticking out of one box and froze.

"Wait, you like BTS?"

"You know them?"

Mei stared at her. "I'm from Singapore, Sara, not another planet."

Sara laughed. "Fair point."

"My bias is Namjoon. Who's yours?" Mei asked excitedly.

"Mine is Jimin," Sara said, joyfully.

"Wow\! And are those anime figures?"

"Maybe."

Mei narrowed her eyes playfully. "Which series?"

"A few."

Something clicked instantly. Within half an hour, they'd moved from biases to music to favorite anime to showing each other their collections of posters and figurines.

Sara hadn't expected to feel comfortable this quickly, but having Mei as a roommate felt like a very lucky start.

By Thursday, Sara had mastered her new commute: Shirokane-takanawa to Tōdaimae. One train, no transfers, roughly thirty minutes.

Her new life was quickly taking shape \- research at the lab, discussions in the seminar, anime chit-chat with Mei, and, somewhere tucked neatly into the middle of it all, Gojo.

His reply from Wednesday finally came through late Thursday evening, while she was organizing her desk.

Gojo: Survived the security incident. Emotionally, I mean.

Sara smiled, typing back immediately.

Sara: You looked deeply betrayed.  
Gojo: I WAS betrayed.  
Sara: But you survived.  
Gojo: Barely.

Sara leaned back in her chair, a soft chuckle escaping her. Across the room, Mei glanced up from her laptop.

"What's with the smile?"

"What smile?" Sara asked defensively.

Mei shrugged, a sly grin playing on her lips. "Nothing."

Sara narrowed her eyes playfully before returning to her screen.

Sara: I'm busy until Saturday, by the way. Properly busy with lab work.

A brief pause.

Gojo: Noted.  
Gojo: Saturday, then.  
Sara: ...  
Gojo: I know a shrine.  
Sara: A shrine?  
Gojo: Trust your guide.

Sara shook her head, a smile tugging at her lips despite herself. She put the phone face-down on her desk and turned back to her reading.

Saturday was still two days away.

And for some reason, that suddenly felt far too long to wait.

Then she remembered a paper she had to finish analysing and jumped back into work.

# **Chapter 7 \- When Will We Reach the Top?**

(Sara's POV)

Friday had tried its absolute best to end Sara. Between her afternoon zemi presentation and her first official nomikai since arriving in Japan, her social battery had collapsed somewhere around the second round of karaoke. Overestimating her tolerance, she hadn't refused a single pouring of sake.

When sunlight finally hit her face, the first thing she heard was Mei's voice from across the room, infuriatingly awake. "You're alive. Barely."

Sara groaned into her pillow. Pieces of the night before were coming back in no particular order \- the zemi, her senior's presentation, someone handing her a drink, someone else insisting on another, then karaoke, then apparently more karaoke. She was fairly sure she'd sung something. She was less sure it had been good.

"I didnt know my roomate Sara had different shades. I hate yesterday's Sara," she muttered.

"She had fun."

"She was irresponsible."

"She was also very loud," Mei said, entirely too pleased about it.

Sara dragged herself upright, which the room did not appreciate, and checked her phone. 2:13 PM. And, worse \- two missed calls.

"You got two calls from Mr Eyemask Tour Guide," Mei added, not even trying to hide her delight.

"You saw that?"

"I see everything. I'm your roommate."

Sara called him back before she could think too hard about it.

Gojo: There she is. I was starting to think you'd been kidnapped.  
Sara: I'm so sorry \- I slept through everything. Tell me what I missed.  
Gojo: Our shrine trip. Remember? Since you clearly slept through sunrise, we're doing sunset instead. 4PM. I'll send the location.  
Sara: Where is it, exactly?  
Gojo: You'll see when you get there. Wear shoes you can actually walk in \- there are stairs.  
Sara: That's an ominous amount of detail for a shrine trip.  
Gojo: Don't be late, Ms Kang.

She hung up and stared at the ceiling for exactly four seconds before the math caught up with her. "I have less than two hours," she announced, "to recover from a hangover, eat something, get dressed, cross half of Tokyo, and apparently climb a mountain."

"It's a shrine."

"He said stairs, Mei. That's basically a mountain with extra steps."

\---

Mei, proving herself the superior roommate, had already produced dumplings and rice by the time Sara had washed her face. "You are not climbing anything on an empty stomach after what you drank last night," she said, pushing the plate toward her, and Sara didn't have the energy to argue.

She ate quickly, fought a losing battle with her hair until she settled for a bun that was, at best, functional, and pulled on a hanbok-inspired top, linen pants, a light jacket against whatever wind waited at the top of wherever she was going, and the only shoes in her closet that could reasonably be called hiking shoes.

"Can I come?" Mei asked from the doorway.

"Apparently this one's just for me." Sara grabbed her bag. "Next time, I promise."

"Special tour?"

"Apparently," Sara said again, and Mei gave her a look that said far more than any actual sentence would have.

"Go," Mei said. "You're going to be late."

"Don't start."

"I didn't say anything."

"You were about to."

Mei was still laughing as Sara hurried out the door.

\---

The metro ride gave Sara plenty of time to regret everything.

The farther the train went, the less it looked like anywhere she'd been with him before \- no crowded shopping streets, no bright signage, just quieter stations and eventually a hillside neighbourhood she hadn't known existed.

How does this man get around this entire city like it's his backyard, she thought, watching the unfamiliar stops scroll by. Where does he even live? Where is Yuji's school, for that matter? Why would anyone build a shrine this far from a station?

By the time she climbed the stairs out of the station, her hangover had curdled into a mildly righteous irritation.

Gojo was leaning against a street post at the end of the block, one hand lifted in an easy wave, like he'd been waiting exactly as long as he wanted to be. He wasn't in his usual dark uniform \- something looser, more casual \- and his blindfold was a different pattern than she was used to, deep charcoal instead of plain black.

"Finally," he said. "Ms. Kang, making it on time. If you'd been even five minutes late, I would've had to start calling you Ms. Late-By-Five-Minutes."

"You picked a location that took me over an hour to reach," Sara said, "and didn't offer so much as a train map. I'm new here. Isn't helping with logistics part of the tour guide job description?"

"My bad." He actually looked like he meant it, which surprised her. "Noted for next time."

\---

They walked down a narrow lane she would never have found on her own \- lanterns strung overhead, faded wooden signs, the kind of street that belonged to the neighborhood rather than to tourists. A few food stalls were still open, steam curling off skewers of dango and the blackened skins of roasting sweet potatoes.

"Yaki-imo," Gojo said, already buying two before she could object. "Autumn tax. Non-negotiable."

The sweet potato was warm enough to burn her fingers through the paper, and worth every second of it.

It was somewhere between the dango stall and the next corner that Gojo glanced at her, paused, and steered her \- without warning \- into a tiny shop selling hairpins and combs.

"What are we doing?"

"Your bun is having a structural crisis."

"You have opinions about hairpins now?"

"I have opinions about everything." He picked up a simple dark wood pin with a few small beads hanging from a thin chain. "This one. It'll hold."

She tried to put it in herself first, but the loose strands weren't cooperating, so he reached over and did it for her \- quick, practical, nothing about it lingering. The little beads made a soft clicking sound settling against the wood.

"There," he said. "Structural integrity restored."

"It's not going to fall out?"

"Nope."

"How do you know?"

"Because I chose it."

Sara laughed and didn't argue, and didn't notice the small, satisfied smile on his face when the beads swung as she turned toward the street again.

\---

The stairs, when they finally reached them, were worse than she'd imagined.

She started strong \- too strong, clearly, because by the fortieth step her legs were filing a formal complaint and her head was reminding her, unhelpfully, of every cup of sake from the night before.

"You're slowing down," Gojo observed, infuriatingly unaffected by the incline.

"I'm pacing myself."

"You're dying."

"I'm pacing myself."

She glanced back at him \- hands in his pockets, not even breathing hard \- and narrowed her eyes. Is he secretly a gymbody under that jacket?

"What?" he said.

"Nothing."

"You're thinking something."

"I'm thinking you're annoying."

"Close enough."

He didn't argue further. He also, she noticed somewhere around step sixty, stopped pulling ahead \- just fell into step a little behind her, close enough that she wasn't climbing alone, far enough that it didn't feel like he was watching her struggle. He never said he was waiting. He just was.

Near the top, the trees thinned out enough that she could see the shrine roofline against the darkening sky, and something about the angle of it, the quiet, the lanterns just starting to glow along the path, made her stop.

"Oh my god."

"What?"

"This is so Sailor Moon."

Gojo blinked at her. "Who?"

"Sailor Moon. The anime. The shrine in it looks exactly like this." She gestured vaguely at the stairs, the gate, the whole atmosphere. "Hikawa Shrine. It's iconic."

"I genuinely have no idea what you just said, but I'm going to assume it's a compliment to my shrine."

"It's not your shrine."

"Tasogare Jinja," he said.

Sara repeated the name quietly. "Tasogare."

"Twilight. More or less." He started walking again, and she fell in beside him. "First time I wandered up here \- mission, years ago, long story \- I didn't even know the name yet. Just thought, What is this place?" A small smile appeared. "Found out later it's got a whole 'who's there' history to it. Something about the light being too dim to recognise anyone's face."

Gojo looked toward the shrine gate, the charcoal blindfold hiding his eyes, though his soft smile was clear. "A place where boundaries blur."

"That's actually a pretty good name for a place like this."

"Right?" He looked ahead again. "And at sunset, it makes even more sense."

The conversation moved on, easy as anything, like it hadn't landed on anything in particular.

At the top, she needed a minute before anything else \- hands on her knees, lungs working overtime. Gojo, insufferably, wasn't even breathing hard.

\---

Once she'd recovered, they stepped up to the shrine itself. Sara clapped, bowed, and prayed for the obvious things \- her health, her research going somewhere, a good life in this city she'd barely started understanding. Nothing more complicated than that.

Afterwards, sitting on the worn wooden steps while the light softened around them, she found herself studying the roofline, the joints, the way the whole structure braced itself into the hillside.

"You'd never see eaves this shallow back home," she said, half to herself. "Korean temples curve up dramatically at the corners, like they're reaching for something. This is flatter. Built to bow against the rain instead of fighting it." She paused. "Sorry. I'm lecturing."

"You are," Gojo agreed.

"You're humouring me."

"I'm listening. There's a difference." He tilted his head toward the structure. "What about the foundations? Same logic?"

She blinked at him. "You actually want to know?"

"I asked, didn't I?"

So she kept going \- longer than she meant to, probably \- and he asked one or two real questions in the middle of it, which mattered more than any amount of polite nodding would have.

She was midway through comparing timber joinery techniques when she realized he'd started humming. Low. Tuneless at first. Then unmistakably a melody.

She blinked. "Wait \- is that BTS?"

"I have no idea what you're talking about. I'm humming. Completely unrelated activity."

"That is literally IDOL. You remembered\!"

"40% retention, remember? Some of it apparently stuck."

She shoved his shoulder, laughing, and the architecture lecture dissolved into something lighter.

By the time they found the viewpoint past the shrine proper, the sun was most of the way down, the sky doing something spectacular and orange over the rooftops of a city she still hadn't fully learned the shape of.

Sara took pictures \- too many, probably \- the hairpin's small beads catching the last of the light every time the wind moved through them.

"Come here," she said, holding up her phone. "One more."

Gojo leaned in, blindfold and all, and she caught both of them in the frame \- her hairpin, his easy smile, the sunset behind them doing most of the emotional work neither of them had said out loud.

She looked at the photo for a second longer than she needed to, and for some reason, didn't delete it the way she usually deleted most selfies.

"Good one," she said.

"Obviously. I'm in it."

She laughed and didn't argue.

# **Chapter 8 \- Not Everyone**

(Satoru's POV)

The breeze picked up right as the sun gave up its last real argument for staying, and the little beads on her hairpin swung with it, catching what was left of the light.

Satoru watched them for a second longer than the view really warranted.

She'd dressed for this as it mattered \- the hanbok-inspired top, the careful modesty of it, nothing flashy, nothing that looked like she'd grabbed it off the floor. Most people treated an old shrine like a photo backdrop. She'd treated it like a place you showed respect to. He hadn't told her to. She'd just done it, the way she did most things, without making a production of the decision.

Noted, he thought, filing it away with everything else he'd been filing away since the sidewalk two weeks ago.

\---

They started down the stairs as the light properly failed, lanterns picking up the slack along the path.

"Question," Satoru said. "Morning person or night owl?"

"Why do you ask?"

"You slept until two in the afternoon today."

"That was a hangover. Those are different categories."

"Are they?"

"Yes." She exhaled, the sound of someone replaying a week she hadn't fully recovered from. "You want to know the actual answer? My course is nothing like I thought it'd be. I expected to start with baby steps, but they asked about my interests and pushed me straight into the real project. I got assigned to a lab and a professor who asks three questions and somehow makes you feel examined without raising his voice once. Everyone in that seminar treats disagreement like a sport. Just thinking about it, I couldn't sleep properly for the first three nights ."

"Sounds like a delightful man."

"He's fine. He's just \- rigid. In a way I wasn't expecting." A small laugh. "So by Friday, when everyone wanted to go drinking after zemi, I thought, why not. And then I thought it again after the first round. That was the mistake."

"The nomikai."

"The nomikai." She shook her head. "At least I've got a decent roommate. Could've been worse."

"Does this decent roommate have a name, or do I just keep calling her 'the roommate'?"

"Mei."

"Noted."

She didn't offer much about herself without being asked; he was noticing. When she did, it came out sideways, folded into something else.

\---

They stopped where the stairs widened into a landing, an old bench nobody used sitting off to one side. Sara dropped onto it, legs clearly grateful for the mercy.

A piece of ash drifted down from somewhere \- a nearby lantern, maybe, or just the air doing what autumn air did \- and landed near his jaw.

She reached up without thinking about it \- an automatic gesture, the kind that didn't ask permission because it never occurred to her that it needed any. He watched her hand come toward his face and did not move.

Her fingers caught the edge of the blindfold instead.

Barely a graze. Just the fabric, just the corner of it. Light enough to be mistaken for nothing at all.

Except it wasn't nothing.

Satoru's reflexes were built to register a threat faster than conscious thought could keep up \- faster than most people's eyes could even track the motion. Some older, simpler instinct should have turned his head an inch to the side before her fingers ever got close enough to matter. People who'd known him for years, people he'd trusted with his life, had gone that whole time without laying a hand on this thing. It wasn't modesty. It was the one boundary that stayed absolute, no exceptions, because what sat behind it wasn't something anyone needed to see.

He didn't move.

For a second that felt considerably longer than a second, there was only the warmth of her fingers against fabric. Then his hand came up and closed around her wrist, just as she'd begun to pull it back on her own \- not to stop her, really, just to hold the moment somewhere he could look at it properly before it disappeared. Under his thumb, her pulse gave one quick, startled flutter.

Not everyone gets this close, he thought, watching her realize, a half-second too late, exactly what she'd done. You didn't even notice you were the exception.

"Careful," he said, and the word came out lower than the joke it was supposed to be.

"Sorry \- there was ash; I wasn't thinking \- "

"I know." He let go, easy, as it cost him nothing. "Didn't peg you for the handsy type, Ms Kang."

She pulled her hand back as it had acted without her permission, which, as far as he was concerned, it kind of had. For the rest of the descent she was very interested in her own footing, cheeks doing something she probably thought wasn't visible in lantern light.

It was.

He didn't push it. Didn't make her explain herself twice. Just let the quiet sit until it got tired of itself.

\---

The restaurant was small, tucked a few streets from the station \- the kind of place that had clearly been serving the same handful of dishes since before either of them existed.

"Do you come here often?" Sara asked, looking over the menu.

"Sometimes. Food's good, nobody bothers me."

"Because of the blindfold?"

"Because I'm charming."

"Right."

They ordered the grilled saury \- a whole fish, skin charred dark off the grill, served with rice, miso soup, and a mound of grated white radish on the side. Sara took her first bite carefully, clearly braced for bones, and went quiet the way she did when something was better than expected.

"Oh. The skin's really crisp."

"That's the idea."

By the time they were halfway through, most of the awkwardness from the stairs had burned off on its own.

It was somewhere around the second helping of pickles that she finally asked the thing she'd clearly been sitting on.

"Okay. Genuine question." She pointed her chopsticks at him. "Not why you wear it, I already know I'm not getting that. How do you actually see anything? Does it just stay dark the whole time?"

"That's classified."

"Everything's classified with you."

"Only the interesting parts." He leaned back. "Short answer \- I see plenty. Just not the way you'd think."

"That's not an answer. That's a riddle wearing an answer's clothes."

"Accurate description, actually."

She narrowed her eyes at him, clearly unsatisfied, and he let the smile hold without giving her anything further. When the dessert menu came around, he ordered without consulting her \- warabi mochi, soft jelly cubes dusted in roasted soybean flour.

"Is everything in this country required to jiggle?"

"Apparently. It's a national law."

She ate it anyway, unconvinced right up until the first bite, after which she stopped arguing entirely.

There was no clock to watch tonight. No gate locking itself at ten. She seemed to notice its absence somewhere around her tea, the shape of an evening that didn't have anywhere urgent to end.

\---

"I've been thinking," Satoru said, once they'd settled the bill, "about your complaint from earlier."

"Which one. I had several."

"The travel one. No lift, no train map, an hour each way on a hangover."

"A valid complaint."

"Extremely valid. So." He gestured toward the street, where he'd already flagged down a cab. "Door to door this time. Compensation."

"You didn't have to do that."

"I know. Get in anyway."

They took a short stroll past the now-quiet lantern street first, the stalls mostly shuttered for the night, before the cab pulled up and they climbed in. It was a longer ride than the walk up had felt, the city sliding by dark and unhurried outside the window.

"So," Sara said, somewhere past the first few minutes of quiet. "Where do you actually live? Like \- what area?"

"Why, planning to move in?"

"I'm planning logistics. I've been dragged to three different corners of this city for you now, and I still don't know where you're starting from. I'd like it if next time we met somewhere in the middle, for once."

"Reasonable."

"So. Area."

"Tokyo," he said. "Somewhere in it."

"That's not an area. That's the entire city."

"It's a big city."

She gave him a look that could have stripped paint. He offered nothing further, entirely unbothered.

"Fine," she said eventually. "Keep your secrets. But next time, middle ground. I mean it."

"Deal."

She didn't push past that. He noticed that about her too \- she asked, and when the answer came back thin, she let it stay thin, rather than treating the question as something she was owed a fuller version of. He wasn't sure yet what to do with the fact that he noticed.

\---

The cab let them off a short walk from the dorm, and he walked her the rest of the way out of habit more than necessity.

"Thank you for today," she said. "All of it. Even the stairs."

"Especially the stairs."

"Goodnight, Gojo."

"Goodnight, Sara."

She went inside, and for once, nobody was timing it.

He didn't usually bring people up to Tasogare. Hadn't, in all the years since he'd first found it. He didn't examine too closely why today had been the exception.

# **Chapter 9 \- You still have the Hairpin?**

After a long day and four floors of stairs, Sara collapsed onto her bed. She glanced around. Mei wasn't in the room. While she replayed how the day had gone, sleep crept up on her, the heavy, dreamless kind that follows too many stairs.

Except it wasn't dreamless.

\---

*Oh, what is this weather? I don't remember coming here, but it looks so whimsical.*

The first thing Sara notices is the sky.

It looks as though the sun has dipped its brush in a few of its favourite shades, apricot and rose and a whisper of lilac, painted them across the canvas of the evening, and wandered off without washing the brush. Beneath it, a lane runs between wooden shopfronts, strung with lanterns in uneven, drifting rows, each one glowing the soft orange of a candle behind paper. Autumn leaves tumble along the stones in no hurry at all. The trees sway as if they're listening to a song only they can hear, and the air smells sweet, with something warm and smoky underneath. Roasted chestnuts.

*What is this cluttered yet peaceful place?*

The lane is busy, and everyone is dressed in traditional clothes, kimono and yukata drifting past like slow, bright fish.

*Why is everyone in traditional dress?*

Then a sleeve brushes her arm, and she looks down.

*Am I wearing a kimono? I don't even own one.*

It's lovely, cream at the shoulders fading into soft yellow at the sleeves, with a brown sash cinched at her waist. Her hand rises to her hair and finds small yellow flowers tucked into her bun.

A sudden glint of light catches her eye from the corner, where a little shop spills lamplight across the stones. Mirrors. Round ones, tall ones leaning against the walls, hand mirrors with lacquered handles hanging in rows, every one of them holding a piece of lantern glow so the whole shop shines like the inside of a lamp. Sara steps in to check herself.

*Who is that?*

The girl in the mirror has a light blush on her cheeks, glossy lips, and a perfect bun crowned with yellow flowers, like a buttercup that has learned to walk. Sara tilts her head. The girl tilts hers.

*Is that really me? I've never looked like this.*

She smiles, and the girl smiles back.

*Oh. I'm pretty.*

A small bag with tassels dangles from her wrist. Inside, a handful of coins.

*I have money\!*

On a shelf near the door sits a small hand mirror with a crescent moon traced along its edge. *This one. I'll take it.*

Outside, a stall sells strawberries on sticks, each wrapped in a shell of sugar as thin as glass.

*Candied strawberries\!*

She buys one. The sugar shatters crisply, and the berry underneath is cold and tart and sweet all at once.

*Well, at least dinner is delicious.*

A deep boom rolls down the lane. The crowd begins to drift in a single direction, faces tipped upward.

*What was that sound? Where is everyone going?*

Another boom. A glimmer of gold above the rooftops.

*Fireworks\! They're starting a fireworks show\!*

*"Ms Kang\!"*

Sara turns. Above the crowd, a head of white hair and a tall figure making his way toward her.

*Wait. Is that Gojo?*

He steps out of the crowd in a black and red yukata, loose over one shoulder in a way his usual uniform never allows. The blindfold is still there, same as always.

*He looks so handsome. I never thought a yukata could suit anyone this much.*

*"Looking beautiful, Ms Kang."*

It isn't a tease. There's no sarcasm wrapped around it, no banter. It sounds like he means it.

His hand lifts toward the flowers in her bun and pauses at something among them. Wooden beads swing softly against her hair.

*"You still have the hairpin I picked."*

*I didn't even notice it was still there.*

The first firework opens above them, and they turn toward it together. Red, then green, then blue, then gold, each burst unfolding like a flower in a night sky with a thin crescent moon, as if someone had scattered a rainbow across the dark.

When Sara turns her head, Gojo is already looking at her, almost as if he's admiring her. Another boom. Their hands touch, and there it is, the same warmth she felt that first evening on the street, his thumb moving slowly across her palm.

The warmth is the strangest part. Not the fireworks or the kimono or the fact that Gojo has appeared as if he'd stepped out of a storybook. It's how natural it feels to stand beside him, fingers brushing hers, watching colours bloom across the night.

*Why am I getting butterflies from one small touch?*

In the light of the fireworks, he looks as if he'd come down from the sky on a ladder of sparks.

Then the crowd begins to surge. People press in from every direction, closer, faster.

*Why is everyone rushing this way?*

*"Hey, don't push me\!"*

A shoulder. An elbow. The lane tilts like the deck of a ship. His hand slips out of hers, and her foot comes down on nothing at all \- 

*"Ahh..."*

\---

Sara woke up on the floor beside her bed, rolled up in her blanket.

*Oh. I fell off the bed.*

It took her a moment to understand that it had been a *dream*. In all of it, she hadn't once wondered whether it was real.

Across the room, Mei was fast asleep, snoring softly, undisturbed. Luckily, no one had seen her tumble out of bed in the middle of the night.

For a second she just lay there, staring at the dark ceiling, her heart doing something embarrassingly specific.

*It's been around a week,* she thought. *I've known him just for a week.*

She pulled the blanket over her face, mortified at absolutely no one. She would think about why in the morning. Her hand was still curled, still faintly warm where his thumb had brushed her palm. She tucked it under the blanket, as if hiding it might make it forget, and was asleep before she could finish being embarrassed.
